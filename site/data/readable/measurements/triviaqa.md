# TriviaQA
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 196  
**Also known as:** TRIVIAQA, TrivialQA, Trivia-QA, TQ  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across papers, TriviaQA is most commonly treated as a reading-comprehension or question-answering benchmark built from trivia questions, usually with short or free-form answers. Definitions differ on whether it is framed as open-domain QA, closed-book QA, or answering from evidence documents: some describe question-answer pairs authored by trivia enthusiasts, while others emphasize question-answer-evidence triples or source documents from Wikipedia and the web. Papers also use named variants and subsets such as TQ, the Wikipedia partition, dev and test splits, a 1000-question dev subset, and a synthesized audio version.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers use TriviaQA to measure 15 constructs and 15 behavioral indicators. The prevailing behavioral uses are question answering and open-domain question answering, followed by factual question answering, factual recall, and text generation, with 10 other behavioral indicators also linked. On the construct side, papers most often use it for world knowledge and factuality, then generalization, factual knowledge, and calibration, with 10 other constructs also linked, so the measurement claims are spread across many targets rather than centered on one.

**Space of uses.** In practice, papers usually deploy TriviaQA alongside Natural Questions, WebQuestions or WebQ, HotpotQA, PopQA, and MMLU in multi-benchmark evaluations. It is commonly positioned as a standard evaluation set for open-domain or factual QA, and for world knowledge evaluations, though some papers use it as an unseen or out-of-domain test set, an in-domain dev benchmark, a retrieval benchmark over Wikipedia passages and documents, a source of questions and answers for dialogue or evidence generation, or training data for fine-tuning. One paper also reports that TriviaQA is related to HELMET, which curated it for long-context benchmarking with 128k input tokens.
