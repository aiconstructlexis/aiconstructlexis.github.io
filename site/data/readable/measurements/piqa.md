# PIQA
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 301  
**Also known as:** PiQA, Piqa  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across papers, PIQA is most commonly described as the Physical Interaction: Question Answering benchmark or dataset for physical commonsense reasoning about everyday objects and situations. Definitions usually present it as multiple-choice question answering, often asking a model to choose the more physically plausible solution or action, and many papers use it in zero-shot evaluation. Some papers also refer to it as "Reasoning about Physical Commonsense in Natural Language"; a few descriptions instead call it a generative QA dataset or use the name "Physical Interaction Quality Assessment," and one paper says it contains 21k questions across different subsets.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers link PIQA to 11 constructs and 11 behavioral indicators. The prevailing claim is that it measures Commonsense reasoning, and it is also repeatedly used to measure Reasoning and Physical world understanding, while the remaining construct links are spread across eight other framings. At the behavioral level, papers most often use it for Zero-shot question answering and Zero-shot task performance, with nine other indicators also linked.

**Space of uses.** In use, papers usually place PIQA inside broader zero-shot or downstream benchmark suites rather than as a standalone evaluation. It is related to three named evaluation toolkits or suites, lm-evaluation-harness, OpenCompass, and OLMES, and papers commonly pair it with HellaSwag and WinoGrande in broader commonsense or reasoning evaluations.

**Concerns raised.** A smaller concern-focused use includes PIQA in a benchmark mix to evaluate the impact of data contamination, under the construct Benchmark overfitting.
