# OpenWebText
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 32  
**Also known as:** Openwebtext, OPENWEBTEXT  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across papers, OpenWebText is most commonly described as a large-scale text corpus or dataset, often an open-source replication of the WebText dataset, used for pre-training and validating language models. Some authors make the content more specific, describing it as a large-scale English, web-scraped corpus, and size estimates differ slightly, with definitions giving approximately 8 billion or 9 billion tokens. A smaller line of work defines it by experimental role, as a source of prompts for sentence continuation or as a text generation dataset for open-ended language completion evaluation.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Its recorded measurement links converge on one behavioral indicator: papers link it to 1 behavioral indicator, Text generation, and to no constructs. This aligns with definitions that describe it as evaluating open-ended language completion performance and with relationship evidence where papers report text generation experiments on OpenWebText or compare unconditional samples on OpenWebText.

**Space of uses.** Papers most often use OpenWebText as training data, especially for GPT-2 style pretraining, and some also use it for validation, including validation perplexity, or as the document source for empirical risk and bound evaluation. Other reported uses are as a prompt source for sentence continuation and as a task dataset for open-ended text completion. In the reported setups it appears alongside the Pile and WIKITEXT103, and one comparison contrasts models trained on WebText versus OpenWebText; no other measurement instrument is linked to it.
