# StrategyQA
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 95  
**Also known as:** STRATEGYQA  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across these papers, StrategyQA is most commonly described as a commonsense question-answering benchmark or dataset built around yes/no, true-or-false, or binary questions that require implicit multi-step or multi-hop reasoning. Definitions repeatedly emphasize inferring a strategy, decomposition, or reasoning path from world knowledge or open-domain questions, and some papers explicitly frame it as an implicit multi-hop benchmark or place it in BigBench. A smaller line of work describes added structure around the questions, including supporting evidence, rationales or explanations, Wikipedia evidence, or, in an agent setting, a curated knowledge base of 10k Wikipedia passages.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers use StrategyQA to measure 10 constructs and 6 behavioral indicators. The prevailing usage is for commonsense reasoning, followed by multi-hop reasoning, reasoning, calibration, and factuality. Taken together, the claims spread across many framings rather than staying within one target, with the remaining links covering 5 other constructs and 6 behavioral-indicator uses.

**Space of uses.** Many papers use StrategyQA alongside other benchmarks rather than on its own, commonly with CommonsenseQA, GSM8K, MuSiQue, QASC, and HotpotQA. They variously position it as a commonsense reasoning benchmark, a multi-hop or open-domain question answering task, a chain-of-thought reasoning task, or a classification-style evaluation set for calibration and adversarial attack testing; some papers also use it as an external dataset, for fine-tuning, or in retrieval-augmented and agent settings. No measurement instrument is linked to it.
