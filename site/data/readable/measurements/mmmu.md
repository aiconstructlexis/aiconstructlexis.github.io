# MMMU
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 109  
**Also known as:** MMMU Validation Set, MMMU Benchmark  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across papers, MMMU is most commonly described as a large multi-discipline multimodal understanding and reasoning benchmark for college- or university-level problems that require expert knowledge and complex reasoning. Definitions vary on scope and size: some describe six core disciplines, others 30 college-level subjects or domains, several specify 11.5K questions, one describes about 12.5K questions, and one notes 30 types of images. Papers also describe different formats and subsets, including VQA or image+text-to-text settings, multiple-choice and free-form questions, both single- and multi-image questions, and dev or validation subsets used for evaluation.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers link MMMU to 14 constructs and 6 behavioral indicators, so its reported use is spread across many abilities rather than centered on only one, though the prevailing framing is multimodal understanding. It is most often used to measure multimodal understanding, reasoning, visual understanding, visual reasoning, and multi-image reasoning, with 9 other constructs also linked. At the behavioral level, papers most often use it for visual question answering, with additional use for diagram understanding, multi-image visual question answering, and other question-answering formats, while one paper reports hallucination as related to MMMU scores rather than measured by them.

**Space of uses.** Most papers use MMMU as one part of broader evaluation suites, commonly alongside MME, MMBench, MathVista, POPE, and SEEDBench, and position it as a general, comprehensive, or challenging check on multimodal capability, reasoning, or expert knowledge. Some papers narrow to particular slices, such as multiple-choice questions from the validation set, scientific-diagram subcategories, or both single- and multi-image questions. Others reuse it as source material by selecting questions or images from MMMU(dev), or by building a culture-agnostic subset from it.

**Concerns raised.** A smaller line of work records caveats about MMMU: one paper notes that a model can achieve 42.7% on MMMU without any visual input, another says the benchmark is dominated by LLM capabilities and largely uncorrelated with other benchmarks, and another describes it as limited to multiple-choice questions with only a small fraction of mathematical content.
