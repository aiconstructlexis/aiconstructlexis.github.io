# OpenBookQA
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 205  
**Also known as:** OpenbookQA, Obqa, openBookQA, OpenBook QA, Openbook QA, OBQA  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Most descriptions frame OpenBookQA as a multiple-choice question answering benchmark or dataset modeled after open-book exams and centered on elementary science questions. Papers usually say it requires reasoning over a provided small set of science or common-knowledge facts, often together with external or general common knowledge, and some further describe it as commonsense, knowledge-intensive, or multi-hop reasoning. A smaller line treats it more broadly as open-domain or scientific-domain QA, and one paper describes OBQA as a VoiceBench subset. Papers also describe its size differently, with one stating 500 multiple-choice questions and others describing 5,957 science questions with four options.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers use OpenBookQA to measure 12 constructs and 8 behavioral indicators. The prevailing framing is Commonsense reasoning, with recurring use for Natural language understanding and Reasoning; at the behavioral level, it is also used for Question answering and Multiple-choice question answering. Other construct and behavioral uses are also reported, so papers do not treat it as tied to only one target.

**Space of uses.** It is related to two other instruments, OLMES and lm-evaluation-harness. Papers commonly place it inside larger benchmark suites, often alongside ARC, PIQA, and HellaSwag, as one task within broader evaluations. It is frequently used in zero-shot evaluation and usually reported with accuracy, though some papers also use it for few-shot evaluation, fine-tuning, held-out generalization tests, or validation.
