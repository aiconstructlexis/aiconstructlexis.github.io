# SEED-Bench
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 60  
**Also known as:** SEEDBench, SEEDbench, SeedBench, SEED  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the papers, SEED-Bench is most commonly described as a comprehensive multimodal or vision-language benchmark for multimodal comprehension or generative comprehension. Many definitions present it as a multiple-choice benchmark, with one recurring description calling it a human-annotated dataset of 19K multiple-choice questions spanning 12 evaluation dimensions across image and video modalities, while another describes a visual question-answering benchmark with 9 dimensions scored by MCQ accuracy. Some authors narrow it to complex visual scene comprehension, broad cognitive tasks such as instance interaction, text understanding, and spatial relations, or video comprehension tasks such as action prediction, action recognition, and procedure understanding; papers also refer to image, video, all, and SEED-Image subsets, and sometimes to the image part simply as SEED.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers link SEED-Bench to 5 constructs and 3 behavioral indicators. The prevailing construct-level use is to measure visual understanding, with additional papers using it to measure multimodal perception, multimodal understanding, reasoning, and visual perception. At the behavioral level, papers most often use it to measure visual question answering, and also multiple-choice question answering and visual instruction following, so the reported measurement claims cover several nearby capabilities rather than one single target.

**Space of uses.** In use, papers usually include SEED-Bench in larger benchmark suites, most often alongside MME and MMBench, and also with MM-Vet, GQA, and VQAv2. The prevailing position is as a general evaluation check for multimodal or vision understanding, though some papers focus on its image part, SEED-Image, or report all, image, and video subsets, and others emphasize its video-oriented questions when testing action prediction, action recognition, or procedure understanding. A smaller line of work uses it as a source of images for another benchmark or curates data from it together with other evaluation datasets.

**Concerns raised.** One paper uses SEED-Bench within a contamination-focused evaluation, reporting that experiments on SEED-Bench and other benchmarks show that a proposed evaluation setup reduces data contamination and exposes performance limitations of LVLMs.
