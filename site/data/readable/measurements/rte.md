# RTE
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 78  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the surveyed work, the dominant framing treats RTE as a textual entailment or natural language inference benchmark built from premise-hypothesis sentence pairs, where the task is to judge whether one sentence entails or can be inferred from the other. Several definitions make the label space explicit as a binary decision, such as entailment versus not entailment or "True" versus "False," and one definition describes it as a collection or series of textual entailment challenges. Papers place it in both GLUE and SuperGLUE, and one paper describes a negation-focused use with versions framed as entailment, contradiction, or neutral.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers link RTE to 3 constructs and 3 behavioral indicators. The prevailing measurement claim is that papers use it to measure Natural language inference, often described as textual entailment; additional papers use it to measure Binary classification and Fact checking. At the construct level, it is used to measure General ability, Natural language understanding, and Language modeling ability, so the reported measurement claims extend beyond a single construct.

**Space of uses.** Papers usually use RTE as one item in larger benchmark batteries, most often alongside GLUE and SuperGLUE task collections and with other tasks such as QNLI, MNLI, BoolQ, SST-2, and COPA. Most position it as part of a broader evaluation suite, though some fine-tune directly on RTE, use it in zero-shot evaluation, or revisit it for in-context learning and prompt robustness analyses; reported results are commonly accuracy or two-way classification accuracy.
