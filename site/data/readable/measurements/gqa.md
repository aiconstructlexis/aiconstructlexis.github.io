# GQA
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 106  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Most commonly, papers treat GQA as a visual question answering benchmark or dataset for real-world visual reasoning and compositional question answering over images. Many descriptions emphasize complex, compositional, or multi-hop questions, and some add structured content: scene graph structures from Visual Genome that generate balanced question-answer pairs with one ground-truth answer, or scene graphs plus a SQL-like reasoning path for each VQA pair. A smaller line of work foregrounds particular splits or variants, including balanced-testdev, test-dev, test-balanced, and GQA balanced, while some papers simply describe it as a public VQA benchmark with open-ended questions.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers use GQA to measure 12 constructs and 3 behavioral indicators, so the reported uses are spread across many targets rather than concentrated on one. The most recurrent framings are visual understanding, compositional reasoning, visual perception, generalization, and visual question answering; the remaining links cover 8 other constructs and 2 other behavioral indicators. In the recorded language of the papers, GQA is also reported as measuring reasoning-oriented abilities in several forms, including multimodal, spatial, relational, and fine-grained visual variants.

**Space of uses.** No other measurement instrument is linked to GQA. In practice, papers usually place it inside broader benchmark suites, often alongside VQAv2, TextVQA, VizWiz, ScienceQA, and POPE, among other benchmarks. It is commonly positioned as a standard or general VQA benchmark, and some papers use it more specifically for out-of-domain evaluation, zero-shot image VQA evaluation, reasoning evaluation, or as a source dataset for derived resources such as MS-GQA and collections of real VQA instances.
