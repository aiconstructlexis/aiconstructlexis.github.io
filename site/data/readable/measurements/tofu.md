# TOFU
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 23  
**Also known as:** TOFU dataset  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the papers, TOFU is most commonly described as a synthetic question-answering benchmark or dataset built from fictitious author biographies or synthetic author profiles. Most descriptions frame it as an unlearning benchmark for author-related knowledge, though some instead describe fictional characters' personal information or personally identifiable information. Papers also refer to splits such as forget05, forget10, and retain90, and they report scales from 200 QA pairs for 10 fictitious authors in forget05 to 4,000 QA pairs from 200 fictitious authors overall, while one paper describes it as 20 question-answer pairs generated by GPT-4.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers use TOFU to measure three constructs and one behavioral indicator. The most often named construct-level uses are Forget quality, General ability, and Verbatim memorization, and the linked behavioral indicator is Knowledge generation. Definitions and quoted passages also describe TOFU as assessing forget quality and model utility, and as evaluating recovery of unlearned author-related knowledge and keywords from fictitious QA pairs.

**Space of uses.** Papers commonly position TOFU as one of several popular or well-established unlearning benchmarks. It is used alongside WMDP in several studies, and also appears with MUSE, Who's Harry Potter, and the Harry Potter series. One paper relates TOFU to MUSE by contrasting TOFU's synthetic Q&A setting with MUSE's focus on real-world unlearning challenges.
