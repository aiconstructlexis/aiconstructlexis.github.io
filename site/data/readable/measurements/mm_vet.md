# MM-Vet
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 83  
**Also known as:** MM-VET, MMVet  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the papers, MM-Vet is most commonly described as a comprehensive multimodal benchmark for integrated capabilities, visual reasoning, and understanding across diverse scenarios. Several definitions make it more specific: it is presented as an open-ended or free-form question answering benchmark, sometimes as an in-the-wild or zero-shot evaluation, and some papers note that scoring is done by GPT-4 through comparative judging of predicted and ground-truth answers. A recurring characterization says it contains 217 challenging open-ended tasks that require multiple capabilities at once, and another says it defines six core vision-language abilities and 16 combinations, including recognition, OCR, knowledge, spatial awareness, language generation, and math; a smaller line of work describes it as evaluating visual conversations.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers use MM-Vet to measure 9 constructs and 4 behavioral indicators, so its reported target is spread across several abilities rather than centered on a single one. The prevailing construct links are visual understanding, multimodal understanding, reasoning, spatial understanding, and fine-grained visual understanding, while smaller lines of work also use it for general ability, knowledge, visual reasoning, and zero-shot generalization. Among behavioral indicators, it is most often used for visual question answering, with additional uses for visual dialogue, text generation, and visual instruction following.

**Space of uses.** Most papers use MM-Vet alongside broader benchmark suites, especially MMBench, MME, MMMU, SEED-Bench, and LLaVA-Bench. In use, it is usually one component of a larger evaluation package for image understanding, visual understanding and reasoning, or multimodal comprehension, though some papers position it as an additional robustness check or as an unseen-task test for zero-shot generalization. Papers also vary in task framing, treating it variously as a VQA benchmark, a benchmark for free-form visual conversations, or a general integrated-capability check.
