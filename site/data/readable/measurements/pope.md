# POPE
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 143  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Most papers describe POPE as a benchmark or dataset for evaluating object hallucination in multimodal or vision-language models through binary questions about whether an object is present in an image, often with short yes/no or true/false answers. The common format is three balanced splits or sampling settings, Popular, Random, and Adversarial, and some definitions say objects not present in the image are sampled under these strategies; several papers also call it a polling-based evaluation, a binary classification query dataset, or a VQA protocol, and one paper describes it as using MSCOCO object annotations. A smaller line describes it more broadly as a hallucination VQA benchmark, one paper labels it multi-choice VQA, and one paper instead expands it as "Positional Object Perception Evaluation" for fine-grained localization and counting.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Across papers, the dominant claim is that papers use POPE to measure object hallucination, and more broadly hallucination, in multimodal or vision-language models. Papers also report it as measuring visual question answering, visual hallucination, and visual understanding, but these are secondary framings relative to hallucination-focused use. Overall, papers link POPE to 10 constructs and 8 behavioral indicators, so its reported targets spread across many uses even though the prevailing usage remains hallucination evaluation.

**Space of uses.** Papers usually use POPE as one benchmark within a broader evaluation suite rather than as a standalone instrument. It is commonly reported alongside MME, MMBench, VQAv2, GQA, and SEED-Bench, and papers position it variously as a hallucination or anti-hallucination check, or as part of broader evaluations of visual or multimodal understanding and general ability. Within those evaluations, papers often report average F1 across the Random, Popular, and Adversarial splits, and some focus on particular settings such as zero-shot object hallucination evaluation or the Popular and Adversarial subsets.
