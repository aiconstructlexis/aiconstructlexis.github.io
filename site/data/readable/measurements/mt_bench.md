# MT-Bench
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 266  
**Also known as:** MT Bench, MT-BENCH, MT-bench, MTBench  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the surveyed work, the prevailing description is of a multi-turn, open-ended conversation benchmark for instruction-following and chat evaluation, usually graded by GPT-4 or another strong LLM judge. Most papers describe 80 human-crafted or predefined multi-turn questions or dialogues, often organized into 8 domains or categories such as writing, roleplay, reasoning, math, coding, extraction, STEM, and humanities, and several papers say these are two-turn items totaling 160 prompts; some papers instead emphasize one-turn plus multi-turn reasoning and instruction-following examples or call it an open-source multi-turn conversation benchmark. A smaller line of work treats MT-Bench as a human-annotated benchmark or preference dataset, noting expert-level pairwise annotations or human preferences over model responses.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers use MT-Bench to measure 23 constructs and 13 behavioral indicators, so claims about what it measures are spread across many targets rather than converging on one. The prevailing claims are that it measures instruction following ability and conversational ability, and papers also use it for generalization; at the behavioral level, the most common uses are instruction following and multi-turn dialogue. Further construct and behavior claims extend beyond these dominant framings.

**Space of uses.** Across papers, MT-Bench is commonly used alongside AlpacaEval, MMLU, Vicuna Bench or Vicuna Eval, and Arena-Hard. Authors usually position it as a widely recognized or widely accepted benchmark for open-ended instruction following, general chat, or alignment, though some papers reuse its prompts, sub-categories, or human-judgment variant for evaluator assessment, routing, or efficiency studies. Papers also relate it to LLM-as-a-judge, and many descriptions present the scoring procedure itself as an LLM judge evaluating model responses.

**Concerns raised.** A smaller line of work discusses the judging setup, with one paper saying that rating completions with another LLM, for example MT-bench, can be cheaper but also "similarly suffers from various biases." Papers also report different agreement levels with human judgments, with one description claiming over 80% agreement and another reporting a 63% human agreement rate.
