# MMBench
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 127  
**Also known as:** MMbench, MM-Bench, MMB  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across papers, the prevailing framing is a comprehensive multimodal benchmark for large vision-language or multimodal models, used across diverse tasks that require perception and reasoning, and sometimes knowledge-based or commonsense questions. Many descriptions present it as a multiple-choice or VQA-style evaluation: one paper describes approximately 3000 multiple-choice questions covering 20 capability dimensions, while others describe thousands of VQA-format questions spanning both perception and reasoning, and several authors explicitly cast it as testing whether a model is an "all-around player." Some authors distinguish English and Chinese variants, including MMBench-Chinese, Dev-EN, and version 1.1, while a smaller line of work refers more broadly to an MMBench-series with additional variants. A minority description emphasizes its answer-robustness setup, stating that MMBench and its CN version use all-round shuffling on multiple-choice answers.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers use MMBench to measure 13 constructs and 5 behavioral indicators, so its measurement claims are spread across many targets. The most frequent construct framings are multimodal understanding and visual understanding, followed by general ability and generalization, while visual question answering is the most frequent behavioral indicator. Beyond these, papers also use it for additional perception-, reasoning-, robustness-, instruction-following-, and forgetting-related evaluations. Across these uses, the dominant pattern is broad multimodal or visual capability assessment, often described in terms of perception and reasoning, with some papers narrowing that to fine-grained abilities or robustness.

**Space of uses.** No measurement instrument is linked to it. In practice, papers usually deploy MMBench alongside other benchmark suites, commonly with MME, MM-Vet, SEED-Bench, POPE, and GQA, and they use it in zero-shot evaluation, general-performance tables, image-understanding or VQA assessments, and efficiency or hallucination studies. Papers typically position it as one component of a broader evaluation package rather than a standalone check, though a smaller line of work also uses it as source material by drawing candidate samples or images from MMBench when building new benchmarks.
