# PG-19
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 34  
**Also known as:** PG19  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across papers, the prevailing description is a long-context or long-form language modeling dataset or benchmark built from Project Gutenberg books. Some authors specify it as an open-vocabulary collection with 11 gigabytes of text from over 28,000 books published before 1919, while others describe evaluation slices such as 100 books, 100 long books, 25 sampled documents, or samples averaging about 69k words, and they use validation, test, held-out, or truncated documents for extended-context next-token or perplexity evaluation.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers link PG-19 to five constructs and no behavioral indicators. The most common measured_by claims use it to measure Length generalization and Long-context understanding, usually through perplexity at varying context lengths or length extrapolation on held-out documents, while other papers use it to measure Continual learning, Fluency, and Language modeling ability.

**Space of uses.** In use, papers most commonly report perplexity, word-level perplexity, or next-token generation on long books and long documents, including streaming or infinite-length settings and context-length sweeps from 4k to 32k tokens. A smaller line of work uses it for latency and decoding speedup benchmarks, and one paper uses 20 PG19 books as continual-learning episodes. Papers also use it alongside The Pile, Wikitext-2, and Wikitext-103, and no other measurement instrument is directly linked to it.
