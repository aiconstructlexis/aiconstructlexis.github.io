# ARC
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 150  
**Also known as:** Arc, AI2ARC, AI2 Reasoning Challenge, AI2-ARC, ARC-easy/challenge, AI2 Reasoning Challenge (ARC)  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Most papers treat ARC as the AI2 Reasoning Challenge, a multiple-choice question answering benchmark of grade-school science questions used to test knowledge and reasoning, often described as difficult for simple retrieval or word co-occurrence methods. Definitions commonly note the ARC-Easy and ARC-Challenge splits, and some papers specify about 7.8k questions or say the questions are drawn from standardized tests. A smaller line of work uses the same name for the Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus or Challenge, a visual or grid-based benchmark in which models infer transformation rules from input-output examples and apply them to a test grid, sometimes framed as abstract or fluid-intelligence evaluation.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers use ARC to measure 8 constructs and 9 behavioral indicators. The prevailing claims center on commonsense reasoning, broader reasoning, question answering, multiple-choice question answering, and machine reading comprehension. Beyond those recurring uses, papers spread ARC across additional reasoning-, ability-, and zero-shot-oriented targets, so it is used for multiple targets rather than only one.

**Space of uses.** Across papers, ARC is related to 2 other instruments, Open LLM Leaderboard and lm-evaluation-harness, and it is usually positioned as one component of broader evaluation suites rather than a standalone protocol. Authors often report it alongside MMLU, HellaSwag, and TruthfulQA, with other companion benchmarks also appearing, and zero-shot evaluation is common. Some papers instead use few-shot or 25-shot settings, average ARC-Easy and ARC-Challenge into one score, or focus only on ARC-Challenge.

**Concerns raised.** Concern-focused discussion is sparse. One paper examines how SMART filtering changes ARC evaluation, another characterizes ARC, alongside similar datasets, as recall-intensive because it largely depends on recalling factual information learned during training, and several descriptions emphasize that the questions are intended to resist simple retrieval or word co-occurrence strategies.
