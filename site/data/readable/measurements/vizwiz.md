# VizWiz
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 72  
**Also known as:** Vizwiz, VisWiz, VizWiz-VQA, VizWizQA, VizWiz test-dev  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the surveyed work, papers most commonly describe VizWiz as a visual question answering benchmark or dataset built from questions asked by blind or visually impaired users about images they have taken. The prevailing description emphasizes real-world or user-generated content, often with uncurated, low-quality, ambiguous, visually degraded, or otherwise challenging images; some papers further frame it as open-ended VQA or natural-image VQA. A smaller set of papers refers to variants or names such as VisWiz, VizWiz-VQA, VizWizQA, and the VizWiz test-dev split, the latter described as containing 8,000 image-question pairs.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers primarily use VizWiz to measure visual question answering, the only linked behavioral indicator. Construct claims are more dispersed: papers also report 8 construct links, with multimodal understanding, visual perception, visual understanding, and fine-grained visual understanding appearing most prominently, and four other construct framings also reported. Overall, use converges on VQA but is also extended to several broader capability labels.

**Space of uses.** Across papers, VizWiz is usually one part of broader VQA or vision-language evaluation suites rather than a standalone instrument. It is commonly evaluated alongside VQAv2, OKVQA, GQA, and sometimes TextVQA, and papers place it in zero-shot, few-shot, four-shot, eight-shot, validation, and test-dev settings. Some authors present it as a general VQA benchmark, while others use it specifically for perception capability, unseen-task generalization, or broader multimodal understanding checks.

**Concerns raised.** When papers discuss caveats, they mostly emphasize the difficulty of the visual input: definitions repeatedly mention real-world, uncurated, low-quality, ambiguous, or visually degraded images. One paper explicitly says VizWiz had not been exposed in the training data in its setup, and another uses it as an unseen task to assess zero-shot generalization.
