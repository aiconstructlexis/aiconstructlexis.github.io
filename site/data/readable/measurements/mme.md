# MME
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 119  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across papers, MME is most commonly described as a comprehensive multimodal or vision-language benchmark for large multimodal language models, covering perception and cognition across 14 sub-tasks or visual question answering tasks. Definitions often specify content such as OCR, color, counting, scene understanding, recognition, commonsense reasoning, and attribute or relation judgments; one paper further describes the 14 tasks as ten perception-related subtasks and four cognition-focused ones, and another calls the benchmark human-annotated. Papers also refer to named components and subsets, including MME-Perception and MME-Cognition, an aggregate score over those two parts, a perception component built from yes/no questions, and a hallucination subset over existence, count, position, and color. A smaller line of work instead frames it as true/false questions or as a benchmark for following single-image instructions.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers use MME to measure 13 constructs and 5 behavioral indicators. The dominant claims frame it as measuring multimodal understanding, visual perception, visual understanding, cognition, and general ability, which matches the common description of the benchmark as assessing perception and cognition. At the same time, papers attach it to several additional constructs and behavioral indicators, so reported measurement claims are spread across multiple labels rather than concentrated on one single construct.

**Space of uses.** Most papers use MME as one component of a larger evaluation suite rather than on its own, most often alongside MMBench, POPE, SEED-Bench, MM-Vet, and MMMU. In that role, papers position it as a comprehensive or general benchmark for reporting overall multimodal performance, and some additionally report the perception and cognition splits, their aggregate score, or the hallucination subset for narrower comparisons.
