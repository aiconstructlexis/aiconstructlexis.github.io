# SST-2
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 118  
**Also known as:** SST2  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across papers, the dominant framing is a binary sentiment classification dataset or benchmark, usually described as Stanford Sentiment Treebank v2 for positive versus negative judgments on movie-review text. Definitions vary slightly on the unit of text, with papers calling it movie reviews, sentences, or, in one description, "872 single sentences extracted from movie reviews"; several also place it within GLUE. A smaller line of work refers to an out-of-distribution version of SST-2 or uses SST2 as a source of out-of-domain samples for another task.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers link SST-2 to 5 behavioral indicators and 5 constructs. The prevailing use is to measure sentiment analysis and, more broadly, text classification; smaller lines of work use it for downstream task performance and 2 other behavioral indicators. At the construct level, papers also use it to measure adversarial robustness and calibration, with 3 other constructs also linked, so the construct-level claims are spread across several targets rather than centered on one.

**Space of uses.** Papers relate SST-2 to 2 other instruments, most commonly GLUE, where it is positioned as one dataset in broader evaluation suites, and less often a word swap attack setup. Across quoted passages, it is commonly used alongside AG News, BoolQ, and IMDb, and many authors position it as one task among several rather than the only evaluation. Other reported uses include serving as in-distribution data, an out-of-distribution test set, a downstream or benign fine-tuning task, and, in unlearning work, a forget set formed from reviews mentioning selected named personalities.
