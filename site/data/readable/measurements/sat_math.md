# SAT-Math
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 7  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Most papers treat SAT-Math as a standardized SAT mathematics benchmark used to evaluate mathematical reasoning under standardized conditions. The definitions differ slightly in granularity: some describe standardized test math problems from the SAT or a benchmark of SAT mathematics problems, others specify that it is composed of multiple-choice questions from the math section, and one paper describes the task coverage as Intermediate Algebra, Probability, and Calculus.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Across the papers, the linkage converges on 1 construct, Mathematical reasoning, and no behavioral indicators. Papers use SAT-Math to measure Mathematical reasoning, describing it as one of the datasets used for math-domain evaluation, a representative evaluation dataset, or part of a collection of mathematical reasoning benchmarks that papers say covers diverse domains, question formats, and difficulty levels.

**Space of uses.** Papers usually report SAT-Math alongside other math benchmarks, most often GSM8K and MATH, and also AQuA or AQuA-RAT, MMLU-Math, and MMLU-STEM. It is often positioned as one component of broader math evaluation, for example as a term in a math-domain average score, while some papers also mention it alongside MATH when highlighting benchmark results.
