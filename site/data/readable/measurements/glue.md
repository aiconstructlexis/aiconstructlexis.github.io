**Type:** Measurement instrument

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the surveyed papers, GLUE is most commonly described as a multi-task benchmark or suite of diverse natural language understanding tasks, often sentence-level, used to evaluate model performance. Definitions usually mention sentiment analysis, textual entailment or natural language inference, paraphrasing, linguistic acceptability, and semantic textual similarity; some papers describe it as a collection of nine tasks, while others refer to eight tasks or subtasks, and many studies use smaller subsets such as four binary datasets, five tasks, or six tasks. A smaller line of descriptions also includes question answering or coreference resolution in what the benchmark covers.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers link GLUE to 8 constructs and 8 behavioral indicators. The prevailing usage is to measure natural language understanding, and papers also use it to measure text classification, natural language inference, sentiment analysis, and general ability. These claims converge most clearly on natural language understanding, though the recorded links also spread across other constructs and task-level indicators.

**Space of uses.** Most papers use GLUE as a primary downstream evaluation benchmark, typically after fine-tuning, and many report either per-task results or an average GLUE score. It is frequently used alongside SuperGLUE and SQuAD, and more occasionally alongside E2E, AdvBench, and GSM-8K. Some papers use the full suite, while others treat it as an auxiliary natural-performance or general-capability check through selected subsets, including settings with four binary datasets, six tasks, eight tasks, or 2-shot examples in context.
