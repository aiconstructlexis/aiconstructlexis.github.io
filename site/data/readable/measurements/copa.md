# COPA
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 38  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across papers, the prevailing usage treats COPA, the Choice of Plausible Alternatives, as a benchmark or dataset for causal, often commonsense causal, reasoning. It is most often described as asking a model to choose the more plausible cause or effect for a given premise or situation, and one paper says it provides "a premise event and a corresponding causal query question along with two choices"; papers variously frame this as a multiple-choice task, a sentence completion task, or a SuperGLUE task. A smaller line of work describes it more broadly as an NLU benchmark, one paper adds counterfactual reasoning, one paper notes a COPA-0-shot setting, and another says the dataset includes 1000 causal questions.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers link COPA to 2 constructs and 5 behavioral indicators. The dominant measurement claims are causal reasoning and commonsense reasoning, with papers explicitly saying it evaluates causal reasoning abilities or placing it under commonsense reasoning. More narrowly, papers also use it to measure causal discovery, downstream task performance, multiple-choice question answering, sentence completion, and zero-shot task performance, so the reported uses concentrate on causal and commonsense reasoning but extend to several broader evaluation behaviors.

**Space of uses.** In practice, papers often position COPA as one task within broader evaluation suites rather than as a stand-alone instrument. It is linked to 1 other instrument, SuperGLUE, and papers describe it as one of several SuperGLUE or NLU tasks used alongside benchmarks such as BoolQ and RTE in fine-tuning, zero-shot, or downstream evaluations. A smaller usage adapts the text-only COPA format into multi-image cause-and-effect questions.

**Concerns raised.** Only one explicit concern is recorded: one paper describes COPA as an earlier benchmark, released prior to 2011 and likely part of LLM training data.
