# TweetEval
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 4  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Papers most commonly describe TweetEval as a multi-task benchmark for tweet analysis on social media, organized as six text classification tasks: emotion, hate speech, irony, offensive language, sentiment, and stance detection. One study states that it "consists of 6 text classification tasks about tweet analysis on social media," while another presents it more narrowly as a benchmark of multiple Twitter-based NLP tasks and uses the emotion recognition and irony detection subsets.

**What it is claimed to measure.** In the recorded relationships, papers use TweetEval to measure 2 behavioral indicators, Emotion analysis and Irony detection, and 1 construct, Natural language understanding. The task-focused uses come from papers that explicitly take its emotion recognition and irony detection components, while another paper reports conducting experiments on TweetEval "for language understanding," so the reported measurement claims span both specific behaviors and a broader construct.

**Space of uses.** Across the cited work, TweetEval is positioned as a benchmark for multi-task or multi-class evaluation rather than as a standalone single-task test. One paper uses it alongside AffectEval as one of "two multi-task benchmarks" for language understanding, and another draws only on the emotion recognition and irony detection subsets. No other measurement instrument is linked to it.
