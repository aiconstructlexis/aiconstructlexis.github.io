# MedMCQA
**Type:** Measurement instrument  
**Supporting papers:** 45  

## State of the Field

**What it is.** Across the surveyed work, the dominant framing is a large-scale, multi-subject medical multiple-choice question answering benchmark or dataset. Most definitions describe it as built from Indian medical entrance examinations, often naming AIIMS and NEET or NEET PG, and one paper explicitly describes it as a 4-way multiple-choice dataset. Smaller variations describe it as a closed-book medical QA benchmark, one component of the medical MIRAGE benchmark, or a QA dataset that provides plain-text explanations.

**What it is claimed to measure.** Papers link MedMCQA to 2 constructs and 3 behavioral indicators. The prevailing claims use it to measure question answering, medical question answering, multiple-choice question answering, and medical knowledge, with papers describing it as assessing clinical domain knowledge or preliminary knowledge grasp through QA tasks. One paper additionally uses it to measure calibration, reporting ECE and accuracy on MedMCQA.

**Space of uses.** No measurement instrument is linked to it. Papers usually place it inside broader evaluation suites, most often alongside MedQA, and also with PubMedQA, MMLU or MMLU-Med, ARC-Challenge, and HotpotQA, or within larger medical and knowledge-intensive benchmark collections. A smaller line of work uses it for specialized setups such as answer-confidence prediction, layer-wise probing, or fine-tuning and evaluation for the medical domain.

**Concerns raised.** Some papers say benchmarks such as MedMCQA may be useful for assessing clinical domain knowledge, but are "not sufficient for evaluating the practical capabilities of models in assisting healthcare professionals in real-world clinical settings." Another paper says medical QA benchmarks including MedMCQA mainly focus on assessing LLMs' preliminary knowledge grasp.
