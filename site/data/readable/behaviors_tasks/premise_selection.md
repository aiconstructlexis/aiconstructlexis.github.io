# Premise selection
**Type:** Behavioral indicator  
**Supporting papers:** 8  
**Also known as:** Premise retrieval  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** The definition describes premise selection as identifying and retrieving relevant existing theorems, definitions, or lemmas from a large library for use in a new proof. In LeanAgent: Lifelong Learning for Formal Theorem Proving, it is framed as the selection of relevant premises, and effective premise selection is described as "a critical challenge in theorem proving."

**Variants.** The described setting is formal theorem proving, where the behavior concerns searching a large library of prior results for a new proof. The description does not specify additional sub-tasks or modalities beyond selecting relevant premises, where the premises can be theorems, definitions, or lemmas.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to it.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** No construct is linked to it as something it evidences. Separately, one paper reports that formal theorem proving is related to premise selection.
