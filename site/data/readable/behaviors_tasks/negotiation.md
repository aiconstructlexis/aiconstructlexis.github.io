# Negotiation
**Type:** Behavioral indicator  
**Supporting papers:** 9  
**Also known as:** Multi-agent negotiation, Negotiation dialogue  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the definitions, negotiation is most commonly treated as a goal-oriented, multi-turn dialogue between parties with competing interests that aims at a mutually acceptable agreement on one or more issues. A recurring formulation emphasizes interactive proposal exchange, sometimes in multi-agent settings, while a smaller framing describes it more specifically as dividing items or resources with an opponent.

**Variants.** Papers use names such as multi-agent negotiation and negotiation dialogue. The settings span multi-turn, multi-issue conflict dialogue, buyer-seller conversations, and game-theoretic resource division; one example prompt asks participants to negotiate over Peppers, Strawberries, and Cherries, and one study places negotiation alongside emotional support and tutoring as a proactive dialogue application.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to negotiation. In these papers, it is elicited directly through interactive dialogue tasks, including multi-turn, multi-issue exchanges and proposal-based resource division, rather than through a named instrument.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** Only one construct is linked to negotiation, Planning, and the only reported construct relationship is a causal claim from Planning to Negotiation. One paper states that without planning agents' suggestions may saturate and no longer increase the collective score, and no other construct link is reported.
