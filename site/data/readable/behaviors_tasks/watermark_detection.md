# Watermark detection
**Type:** Behavioral indicator  
**Supporting papers:** 12  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage is a binary decision task over a text sequence, suspect text, or generated content: determining whether it contains a watermark signal or was generated by a watermarked model. Success is usually framed as outputting watermarked versus not, often through a thresholded statistic, for example when the average watermark logit is greater than zero, when a z-value exceeds a threshold, or when the text is judged more likely under a marked than an unmarked distribution. Some papers phrase the alternative class specifically as human-generated text.

**Variants.** Papers study several closely related formulations, including text sequence detection, suspect or candidate text detection, and detection from generated content. Some explicitly describe it as the inverse problem of watermark embedding, some recover a key sequence from the content during detection, and others cast it as a statistical hypothesis test over a model and watermarking key. The decision labels vary slightly, with papers using watermarked versus unwatermarked, generated by the watermarked model versus not, or watermarked versus human-generated.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** The papers do not connect this behavior to any named measurement instrument. Instead, they operationalize it with detector algorithms and thresholded statistics: a recurring scoring pattern is a z-score or other hypothesis test, alongside average token-level watermark logits, counts of green list tokens, neural network classification based on whether a z-value crosses a threshold, and cutoff rules over a test statistic. The resulting score is typically converted into a binary detection decision.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** The papers do not connect this behavior to any construct. They therefore do not state a shared construct that watermark detection is evidence of.
