# Multi-agent collaboration
**Type:** Behavioral indicator  
**Supporting papers:** 4  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the prevailing usage treats multi-agent collaboration as observable coordination among multiple agents working toward a collective or task-oriented goal. Most descriptions emphasize collective deliberation, communication, and joint output generation, including cross-examination and debate; some papers add that agents are specialized and that collaboration can reveal emergent abilities and combine specialized expertise. A smaller formulation describes it as generating task-oriented outputs from interaction among agents and external knowledge sources.

**Variants.** Papers study it in several settings. The most explicit variants are collaboration through cross-examination and debate, collaboration among specialized agents, and graph-level tasks where agents communicate with one another and with external knowledge sources to produce task-oriented outputs. One paper narrows this further to answering medical questions through agent interaction with external knowledge, and another relates multi-agent collaboration to iterative response improvement in reasoning tasks with self-generated data.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to it. Instead, papers describe the behavior directly in task terms, such as debate, communication among agents, joint output generation, and interaction with external knowledge.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** No construct is linked to this behavior as something it evidences, so the papers do not specify a shared construct that it measures. Separately, one paper reports that multi-agent collaboration is related to self-correction, describing a multi-agent LLM system trained to iteratively improve its responses for reasoning tasks with self-generated data.
