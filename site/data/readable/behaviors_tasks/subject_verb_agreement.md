# Subject-verb agreement
**Type:** Behavioral indicator  
**Supporting papers:** 5  
**Also known as:** Subject-verb agreement judgment  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, subject-verb agreement is treated as performance on grammar-sensitive outputs: either choosing the appropriate verb inflection from the grammatical number of the subject, or giving correct acceptability judgments for sentences that test agreement. One study says that "the model's task is to choose the appropriate verb inflection" (Sparse Feature Circuits: Discovering and Editing Interpretable Causal Graphs in Language Models), while another frames it through acceptability judgments and includes examples such as "The sisters bake/*bakes."

**Variants.** Papers use two related formulations. One is a suite of subject-verb agreement tasks, and another is subject-verb agreement judgment, abbreviated as "S-v agr.," with a recurring focus on sentences containing attractor nouns; one paper specifically notes that attractors in a relative clause can hinder acceptability judgments.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to it. In the papers, it is elicited directly through agreement tasks and acceptability-judgment items rather than through a named instrument.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** The papers do not link this behavior to any construct, so they do not specify what construct it is taken to indicate.
