# Medical visual question answering
**Type:** Behavioral indicator  
**Supporting papers:** 11  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across papers, the prevailing usage treats it as answering questions about medical images by combining a visual image with a textual question and generating an answer. One paper states that MedVQA "aims to use both visual images and textual questions as inputs to generate the answers" (FEDMEKI: A Benchmark for Scaling Medical Foundation Models via Federated Knowledge Injection). Some authors make the output more explicit as an accurate textual answer based on the image content, and a smaller line of work specifies open-ended, closed-form, or closed-ended answer settings.

**Variants.** Papers use several closely related names, including medical VQA, MedVQA, MVQA, Med-VQA, and biomedical VQA. Most formulations stay at the level of question answering over a given medical image, while one definition expands the task range from simple pattern recognition to complex diagnostic reasoning, and some papers distinguish open-ended from closed-form or closed-ended settings.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** Across papers, this behavior is measured by 6 instruments, all framed as medical or biomedical VQA datasets or benchmarks. The most frequently linked are VQA-RAD and SLAKE, followed by PathVQA, IU-Xray, and MIMIC-CXR, with 1 other instrument. MedTrinity-25M: A Large-scale Multimodal Dataset with Multigranular Annotations for Medicine describes this usage directly: models are benchmarked on "three biomedical Visual Question Answering (VQA) datasets, VQA-RAD, SLAKE, and PathVQA."

**What it is taken as evidence of.** Across the cited papers, it is not linked to any construct, so there is no reported construct that it is taken to evidence.
