# Commonsense question answering
**Type:** Behavioral indicator  
**Supporting papers:** 61  
**Also known as:** Common-sense question answering, Commonsense completion, Commonsense reasoning, Commonsense inference  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across papers, the dominant framing is answering questions that require real-world, everyday, or implicit background knowledge, often contrasted with specialized expertise. The most common setup is multiple-choice question answering on benchmarks such as CommonsenseQA, PIQA, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, and ARC, with success defined as selecting the correct or most plausible answer; some papers describe each item as having one valid option among a small set of choices. A smaller line of work broadens the behavior to sentence completion or plausible continuation selection, and some formulations use binary or true or false answers, including even-or-odd year questions.

**Variants.** Papers study several variants under nearby names, including common-sense question answering, commonsense completion, commonsense reasoning, and commonsense inference. Beyond standard multiple-choice QA, variants include choosing the more plausible of two options in XCOPA, choosing a plausible cause or effect from a pair of choices, and completion-style settings where the model selects the most plausible continuation; some papers also use commonsense-based reading comprehension questions. Settings vary between zero-shot and few-shot evaluation, and one paper contrasts answering from questions alone with answering from questions plus some provided evidence.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** Papers measure this behavior with 5 instruments: CommonsenseQA, HellaSwag, PIQA, StrategyQA, and WinoGrande. These are benchmark datasets used to elicit or score answers, usually in zero-shot or few-shot evaluation and commonly reported by accuracy; CommonsenseQA is described as a multiple-choice daily-life knowledge task, StrategyQA as multi-step and also true or false QA, while HellaSwag, PIQA, and WinoGrande recur as benchmark commonsense reasoning tasks.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** At the construct level, papers make 1 explicit link, to Commonsense reasoning. The recorded relationship is that commonsense question answering is related to Commonsense reasoning, and Beyond Surface Simplicity: Revealing Hidden Reasoning Attributes for Precise Commonsense Diagnosis states that answering commonsense questions correctly requires not only knowledge but also reasoning. Papers do not attach this behavior to other constructs.
