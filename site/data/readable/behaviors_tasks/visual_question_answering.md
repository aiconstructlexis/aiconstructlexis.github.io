## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed work, the prevailing usage is answering a natural language question about visual content, usually a given image, by producing a textual response from the image and the question. Papers often describe an image and an associated question that the model is expected to answer, sometimes in a short-answer format, and some definitions explicitly cover questions about objects, attributes, relationships, or scene text. A smaller line of work broadens the input to one or more images, 3D scenes, website screenshots, embedded webpage images, or video, and the response format varies across open-ended answers, multiple-choice answers, and yes or no answers.

**Variants.** Most papers stay with general VQA or image question answering on single images. Smaller lines of work use names such as general visual question answering, simple question answering, single-image question answering, region-text interleaved VQA, and scene text question answering; other settings specialize the same behavior to GUI screenshots, embedded webpage images, medical images, 3D scenes, industrial object analysis, or visually prompted regions. One paper also distinguishes questions that are agnostic to the image from image-specific prompts, calling them VQAgeneral and VQAspecific.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** Papers measure it with 37 linked instruments, most often VQA-v2, GQA, VizWiz, TextVQA, and OK-VQA. These instruments are usually benchmark datasets, while the broader measurement landscape also includes multiple-choice and yes or no VQA benchmarks, document and medical VQA datasets, 3D scene and video QA datasets, and one LLM-as-a-judge setup that checks whether a predicted answer is semantically equivalent to the ground truth. Scoring is therefore mixed across open-ended, short-answer, multiple-choice, and yes or no formats.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** Papers do not converge on a single construct that this behavior reflects. Among 12 linked constructs, visual question answering is most often related to multimodal understanding and visual perception, and one paper treats VQA accuracy as a proxy for perception capability. Smaller lines of work claim a causal relationship from visual understanding to visual question answering and from visual question answering to brain alignment, while other papers relate it to compositional reasoning and several additional constructs.
