# Modular arithmetic
**Type:** Behavioral indicator  
**Supporting papers:** 8  
**Also known as:** Modular addition  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed work, the prevailing usage treats modular arithmetic as performing arithmetic in a system where values wrap around at a modulus. Definitions usually describe arithmetic operations in general, and one paper makes that scope explicit as addition or counting within the wrap-around system. A narrower formulation appears as modular addition, described as computing the remainder of a sum when divided by a modulus, with outputs also characterized in terms of numbers congruent to a + b mod c, for example whether the answer is even or odd.

**Variants.** Papers mainly study wrap-around arithmetic through addition and counting. The named variants are modular addition and modular counting, and one source also refers more generally to "both modular arithmetic tasks" without further separating them.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No paper links modular arithmetic to a measurement instrument. Accordingly, no named instrument recurs as a way to elicit or score it.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** Only one construct is linked to modular arithmetic. The recorded relationship is to State tracking ability, and it is framed as related rather than as a measurement claim: one paper reports testing methods on parity, modular arithmetic, and permutation composition while "demonstrating improved state-tracking performance."
