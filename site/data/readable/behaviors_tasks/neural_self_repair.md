# Neural self-repair
**Type:** Behavioral indicator  
**Supporting papers:** 7  
**Also known as:** Self-repair  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** The paper treats neural self-repair, under the name self-repair, as a latent mechanism in which a model compensates for changes or losses in individual functional components so that task performance stays stable. In the source description, this appears in perturbation or ablation settings: self-repair is said to plausibly contribute to "behavioral stability," and "negative heads" are described as performing self-repair by reducing their negativity when NMHs are ablated.

**Variants.** The usage in the papers does not separate multiple domains, modalities, or named sub-tasks. It is discussed under the name self-repair in the specific setting of ablation, where the example given is compensation by negative heads after NMH ablation.

**Space of elicitation and scores.** No measurement instrument is linked to it. In the description, it is characterized through ablation-related behavior, namely stable task performance after changes to functional components and reduced negativity of negative heads after NMH ablation.

**What it is taken as evidence of.** No construct is linked to it. The paper connects it to maintaining stable task performance and says it plausibly contributes to behavioral stability, but it does not explicitly treat it as evidence of a linked construct.
