# Visual robustness
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 4  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** In the one definition, visual robustness is framed as the degree to which a model's predictions remain stable and accurate when visual inputs are degraded or distorted, with reliable integration of visual cues included in that framing. The same usage describes it through maintaining consistent prediction patterns across varying visual qualities and through consistent predictive behavior for distorted and normal visual inputs, so the emphasis stays on prediction consistency under changes in visual quality.

**Space of facets.** Papers offer little internal systematization of this construct. One paper places it as a dimension used to examine visual perception capabilities, alongside visual hallucinations and basic visual perception, and another explicitly links confidence calibration to it; no further subtypes or alternative names are defined.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to it, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. The operational language instead describes it through model behavior: predictions remaining stable and accurate under degraded or distorted inputs, consistent prediction patterns across varying visual qualities, and consistent predictive behavior for distorted and normal visual inputs, without a named benchmark, human evaluation, judge model, probe, or analysis being linked.

**Links.** Papers connect it only through related links. One paper relates visual perception to visual robustness by treating visual robustness as one of the dimensions used to study visual perception capabilities, and another relates calibration to visual robustness by explicitly linking confidence calibration to it; papers do not report causal or correlational links for this construct.
