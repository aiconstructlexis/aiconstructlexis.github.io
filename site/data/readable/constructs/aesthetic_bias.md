# Aesthetic bias
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 6  
**Also known as:** Stereotype bias  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Papers do not use a single framing consistently. The direct aesthetic-bias framing is narrow: it is described as subtle, systematic differences in a model's aesthetic judgments that correlate with identity attributes such as gender, age, or education, even when the image content is unchanged. A broader framing also appears repeatedly in related definitions, where the construct is treated as a latent tendency to reproduce, endorse, or agree with stereotypes about social groups or protected attributes; along this axis, papers differ on whether the phenomenon is specific to aesthetic judgments or a more general stereotype-producing disposition.

**Space of facets.** Across the papers, the clearest organizing theme is demographic or protected-attribute bias. One recurring usage places aesthetic bias under broader bias language, calling it a form of subtle bias, while several definitions use stereotype bias for closely related phenomena. The strongest overlap appears when papers describe models assigning different aesthetic judgments based on fixed assumptions about identity groups and showing subtle yet persistent stereotypical biases in emotional aesthetic judgment.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to it, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. Where papers describe how the construct would appear in behavior, they point to models giving different aesthetic judgments for unchanged images when only demographic attributes vary, generating content that perpetuates stereotypes, or agreeing with stereotypical statements, phrased in one paper as computing "the fraction of times the model agrees with the stereotypical statements."
