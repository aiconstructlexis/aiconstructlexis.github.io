# Collaborative reasoning
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 6  
**Also known as:** Collaborative decision-making  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the definitions, the concept is consistently framed as a multi-agent ability expressed through structured debate: multiple agents collectively analyze a problem, advocate and critique possible solutions, and converge on a judgment or decision. M-MAD: Multidimensional Multi-Agent Debate for Advanced Machine Translation Evaluation describes it as a latent ability of multiple LLM agents to reach a more accurate judgment, while SWE-Search: Enhancing Software Agents with Monte Carlo Tree Search and Iterative Refinement uses the label collaborative decision-making for the ability of a multi-agent system to arrive at a robust decision through debate.

**Space of facets.** The papers organize the concept around debate functions: agents advocate for candidate solutions, critically evaluate them, and then refine or select an outcome. The main variation is in naming and end point, with one usage centered on collaborative reasoning and convergence to a more accurate judgment, and another on collaborative decision-making and selection of a robust solution; one quoted passage also places collaborative abilities alongside LLMs' 'knowledge' and 'reasoning' capabilities.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to it, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. Operationally, papers describe it through the debate process itself, structured exchanges among multiple agents that analyze a problem, critique alternatives, and converge on a judgment or decision, rather than through a benchmark, human evaluation, LLM-as-a-judge setup, probe, or analysis.

**Links.** No papers report causal, correlational, or other related links involving this construct.
