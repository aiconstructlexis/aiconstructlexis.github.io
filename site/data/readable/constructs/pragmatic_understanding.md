# Pragmatic understanding
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 16  
**Also known as:** Pragmatic comprehension, Pragmatic inference, Pragmatic knowledge, Pragmatic reasoning  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across papers, the prevailing usage treats pragmatic understanding as a latent, context-sensitive ability to go beyond literal meaning by integrating speaker intent, shared or world knowledge, and social or cultural conventions. Closely related definitions describe understanding how context shapes meaning in conversation, deriving implicatures, interpreting indirect requests and non-literal meanings, and grasping implied or rhetorical functions; one paper summarizes it as deriving conclusions about meaning from contexts, intentions, and language use. A smaller line of work shifts the framing from an ability to the model's internal pragmatic knowledge, defined as representations of how context affects interpretation, including politeness, discourse, and speaker intent, and one application-specific definition narrows it to inferring an asker's underlying information need from ambiguous or underspecified questions.

**Space of facets.** Papers use several neighboring labels, including pragmatic understanding, comprehension, inference, knowledge, and reasoning, for closely related formulations of context-dependent meaning. The clearest axis of variation is whether it is treated as a latent reasoning ability or as internal model knowledge, and a smaller difference is scope, with most definitions covering conversational meaning broadly while one narrows it to ambiguous or underspecified questions. Where papers organize the phenomenon, one groups pragmatics alongside morphology, syntax, and semantics and places discourse and pragmatic markers together, while another treats pragmatic as one subtype within a broader text-based reasoning taxonomy.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is sparse in the linked measures: no measurement instrument is linked to this construct, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. Papers instead discuss it through task settings such as implicature comprehension or resolution, interpreting indirect requests and non-literal meanings, mapping norm definitions to conversations through pragmatic inferences, understanding implicit toxic language, and reconstructing an asker's underlying information need from ambiguous questions. These task descriptions mostly line up with the dominant definitions by centering context, intent, and meaning beyond literal content.

**Links.** Its recorded links are to Logical reasoning, Natural language reasoning, and Theory of mind. One paper claims a causal relationship from Theory of mind to Pragmatic understanding, stating that for humans pragmatic inferences often rely on Theory of Mind abilities. Separate papers report that Pragmatic understanding is related to Logical reasoning, with an observation that high logical-reasoning performance may come at some cost to pragmatic inference, and related to Natural language reasoning, where pragmatic appears as one subtype in a text-based reasoning taxonomy.
