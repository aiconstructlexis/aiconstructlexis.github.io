# Empathy
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 23  
**Also known as:** Emotional support quality, Empathic understanding  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the literature, the prevailing usage treats empathy as a capacity or tendency to understand another person's feelings and perspectives and to respond in ways that show understanding, compassion, resonance, or emotional support. This appears in formulations such as understanding users' emotions in dialogue, showing empathy toward a narrator, aligning with a user's subjective experience, and considering others' perspectives and feelings when addressing issues. A narrower line defines empathy as emotional alignment, that is, how a model's feelings change in specific situations and whether those changes match what humans would feel.

**Space of facets.** Papers do not use a single internal structure for the concept. One recurring account describes empathy as a complex, multidimensional construct whose facets can be treated as a trait, a state, or a social function in conversation, and it explicitly names empathic concern and personal distress as primary facets. In counseling-oriented evaluation, some authors separate a broader notion of emotional support quality from a narrower empathic understanding, defined as reflecting feelings and aligning with the user's inner emotional world, which shows that adjacent labels are sometimes used for different scopes.

**Space of measurement.** The explicit measurement picture is narrow: papers link empathy to 1 measurement instrument, G-Eval, and to no behavioral indicators. G-Eval is used as an LLM-based evaluation metric that scores model outputs on qualitative aspects including empathy. This operationalization is output-focused, whereas some definitions instead describe empathy as models' feeling changes in situations or as a broader multidimensional psychological construct.

**Links.** Papers record no causal or correlational claims for empathy. Instead, papers record related links with 3 constructs. Empathy is related to helpfulness and to personal distress, and empathic concern is related to empathy. One advice-oriented study says the main factor in helpful versus unhelpful advice is empathy, while the theoretical account treats empathic concern and personal distress as facets of an empathy construct.
