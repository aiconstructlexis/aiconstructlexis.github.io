# Semantic diversity
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 4  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, semantic diversity is most commonly described as variation in meaning across generated responses, sentences, or documents. One formulation explicitly adds variation in expression and contextual appropriateness, presenting it as a broader exploration of the response space, while another emphasizes richness and unpredictability in conceptual expression and treats greater diversity as less semantic consistency.

**Space of facets.** The construct is organized mainly through neighboring notions rather than explicit subtypes. Papers relate it to broader exploration of the response space, and one study contrasts it with semantic similarity, described there as semantic consistency, so that lower similarity is used to mark higher semantic diversity.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is sparse but concrete. Semantic diversity is linked to 1 measurement instrument, ConflictingQA, a benchmark of open-ended questions on controversial topics chosen because multiple valid answers from different perspectives are possible, and it has no linked behavioral indicators; another study operationalizes it through semantic similarity analysis, reporting that "HWT exhibit a lower mean similarity than most models, indicating greater semantic diversity" (Linguistic and Embedding-Based Profiling of Texts Generated by Humans and Large Language Models). These operationalizations focus on variation in meaning across valid responses or texts, matching the way the construct is defined in the papers.

**Links.** Relational evidence is limited. One paper, Language Guided Skill Discovery, claims a causal relationship from Semantic understanding to Semantic diversity, hypothesizing that leveraging the semantic knowledge of LLMs can "improve semantic diversity of resulting behaviors".
