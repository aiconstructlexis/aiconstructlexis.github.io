# Formal linguistic competence
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 3  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the descriptions, the construct is framed as the ability to produce fluent, grammatically correct text from statistical patterns in training data, rather than from deep semantic understanding. The only explicit definition further characterizes it as surface-level "data" memory, and a quoted passage says it "mainly comes from statistical learning during training on large text corpora" (Memorization ≠ Understanding: Do Large Language Models Have the Ability of Scenario Cognition?).

**Space of facets.** The framing organizes the construct through a contrast between surface linguistic form and deeper meaning. It is described through fluent and grammatically correct text generation, while being set against "deep semantic understanding" and tied to surface-level "data" memory.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is benchmark-based: papers link formal linguistic competence to 3 measurement instruments and no behavioral indicators. BLiMP and SyntaxGym are the most recurrent instruments, with papers stating that they use these benchmarks to assess formal linguistic competence; another paper also includes GLUE among benchmarks that measure it. These measures follow the same linguistic-ability framing used in the definitions.

**Links.** Beyond measurement, the only linked construct is brain alignment. A paper claims a causal relationship from formal linguistic competence to brain alignment, stating that it "actively drives brain alignment during the early phases of training" in From Language to Cognition: How LLMs Outgrow the Human Language Network.
