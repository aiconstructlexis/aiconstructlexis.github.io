# Proactivity
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 4  
**Also known as:** Proactiveness  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed work, proactivity is consistently described as taking initiative in dialogue, including planning ahead and guiding the user or the conversation flow toward a goal rather than only passively responding or reacting to user inputs. The main variation is in wording rather than substance: some authors frame it as an agent's "tendency," while others describe it as an "ability"; one paper summarizes it as behavior that "proactively guides the user toward the dialogue goal" (ChatSOP: An SOP-Guided MCTS Planning Framework for Controllable LLM Dialogue Agents).

**Space of facets.** Papers mainly organize the concept by contrast: initiative, planning ahead, and guiding the conversation are set against passive response or reaction-only behavior. One paper also discusses proactivity alongside transition-awareness in a framework for "proactive and transition-aware conversational agents," but the definitions do not introduce further named facets within proactivity itself.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument or behavioral indicator is linked to it. The quoted passages nonetheless show one paper assessing dialogue controllability and proactivity through the "accuracy of proactive actions (Acc P)," alongside the accuracy of single-turn dialogue actions and controllable actions.

**Links.** The only recorded construct-level link is a causal claim from Contextual understanding to Proactivity. In Proactive Agent: Shifting LLM Agents from Reactive Responses to Active Assistance, context awareness is said to "enable[] the proactive agent to interpret signals and proactively propose and execute tasks without explicit human instructions."
