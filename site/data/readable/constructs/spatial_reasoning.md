# Spatial reasoning
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 98  
**Also known as:** Visual-spatial reasoning, Spatial cognition, Spatial localization ability, Spatial intelligence, Directional reasoning, Spatiality, Spatial relation inference  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across papers, the prevailing usage treats spatial reasoning as a broad ability to understand spatial relationships, positions, and layouts, often in 2D or 3D environments, visual scenes, or navigation tasks. Recurring formulations emphasize understanding directions, navigating a world, capturing relations like left or in front, inferring relative positions and object size, and reasoning over scene or flowchart structure. A smaller line of work broadens the label to spatial cognition or spatial intelligence, adding perception, mental representation, interaction, and action in space and time, while other papers narrow it to spatial localization from language, directional reasoning within a consistent reference frame, yes or no spatial relation inference under ambiguity, or spatiality as an a priori understanding of Euclidean structure.

**Space of facets.** Papers do not converge on a single taxonomy, and instead use overlapping labels such as visual-spatial reasoning, spatial cognition, spatial intelligence, directional reasoning, and spatial relation inference; a few descriptions further narrow the idea to localization or a priori Euclidean knowledge. One paper divides spatial cognition into large-scale and small-scale forms, and another says spatial reasoning is only a subset of physical world understanding. Domain-specific formulations then specialize it to route structure, flowchart topology, or integrating disjoint egocentric views into a coherent 3D scene representation.

**Space of measurement.** Papers link spatial reasoning to 11 measurement instruments and 8 behavioral indicators. The linked instruments are predominantly benchmark-style tasks: VSR, BLINK, Bongard Problems, GQA, and Maze are the most frequently linked, and their descriptions operationalize the construct through visual spatial reasoning, spatial components of question answering, or interaction with spatial layouts; several other instruments cover 3D worlds, pathfinding, and game-style reasoning. These operationalizations mostly align with the dominant definitions around relations, positions, layouts, and navigation, although some papers also study narrower settings such as flowcharts, articulated objects, or disjoint egocentric frames.

**Links.** Papers do not report a correlational link. One paper claims a causal relationship from spatial reasoning to Generalization, and another from spatial reasoning to Motion planning; separate papers claim causal relationships from Depth estimation to spatial reasoning and from Analogical reasoning to spatial reasoning. Papers also relate it to Geometric reasoning and several other constructs and task settings.
