# Conceptual knowledge
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 6  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the cited work, the prevailing usage treats conceptual knowledge as a model's internal representation of concepts, rather than as simple factual triples. One definition frames it as the representation of abstract concepts and their definitions, explicitly distinguishing it from factual knowledge, while another describes concepts as human-understandable groups of features, examples, or words that share a common property and can be localized in parameters or activations. The common ground is the focus on internal concept representations, with variation in whether the emphasis falls on abstract definitions or on localizable groups of shared features and examples.

**Space of facets.** Papers mainly organize the construct by contrasting it with factual knowledge. In an editing setting, authors state that they used "two types of knowledge for editing: factual knowledge and conceptual knowledge" and report that conceptual knowledge is "more abstract and more difficult to edit than factual knowledge." Another usage describes the underlying unit as a concept understood as a group of features, examples, or words sharing a common property.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument or behavioral indicator is linked to it. Operationally, a paper says that, for conceptual knowledge, the ConceptEdit dataset was adopted in an editing evaluation, and another paper frames the construct through concept erasure, asking whether knowledge about a target concept can be removed so that the model can no longer generate information about it.

**Links.** A paper reports that conceptual knowledge is related to Conceptualization. In that usage, the ability to capture conceptual knowledge is discussed alongside evidence for models' ability to conceptualize.
