# Social and emotional intelligence
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 3  
**Also known as:** Social/emotional intelligence, Socio-emotional skills  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed work, the prevailing usage treats this as an ability, or latent ability, to process social and emotional information in interaction. Definitions converge on understanding social and emotional context, with one paper specifying others' intentions, actions, feelings, and social nuances, and another operationally narrowing it to answering questions about social interactions in ways that reflect such understanding. Another variant uses the label "socio-emotional skills" and includes managing, not just understanding, the social and emotional aspects of interaction; one paper also characterizes human social and emotional intelligence as requiring both introspection and predicting the feelings of others.

**Space of facets.** Papers use closely related labels, including "social and emotional intelligence," "social/emotional intelligence," and "socio-emotional skills," for overlapping ideas. One paper contrasts social/emotional intelligence with physical commonsense as another domain language models do not capture well, while another discusses it in the same neighborhood as theory of mind and executive skills.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is entirely through instruments rather than behavioral indicators: papers link the construct to 2 measurement instruments, most often SIQA, then EQ-Bench. SIQA is described as the Social IQA dataset, and one study states that it "measures social and emotional intelligence through questions about social interactions"; another paper places EQ-Bench within a socio-emotional evaluation grouping. No behavioral indicators or other constructs are linked to it.
