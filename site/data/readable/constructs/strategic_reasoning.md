# Strategic reasoning
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 10

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the definitions, the prevailing framing treats strategic reasoning as a multi-agent, often rule-based ability to analyze competitive situations, anticipate opponents, and formulate plans that achieve goals. Most descriptions stay close to opponent modeling and counter-strategy formation, referring to opponents’ policies, intentions, behavioral patterns, or next moves. Smaller lines of work broaden it to navigating dynamic environments under uncertainty and aligning long-term goals, or narrow it to domain-specific settings such as multi-step red-teaming that exploits policy ambiguities and conflict-resolution strategies consistent with human negotiation behavior. One paper further singles out long-term strategic reasoning as a latent ability to identify multi-step plans that secure future wins rather than merely respond to immediate threats.

**Space of facets.** The clearest subtype is long-term strategic reasoning, presented as finding and executing multi-step strategies, such as positions that guarantee future wins, instead of reacting only to immediate threats. In some game-oriented papers, strategic reasoning appears alongside logical and spatial reasoning, while in dispute-resolution work it appears alongside emotional sensitivity. Closely related labels in the papers include strategic planning, strategic behavior, and counter-strategy formation, but they are used in different task settings rather than explicitly collapsed into a single shared taxonomy.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is narrow in the linked literature: papers connect the construct to 2 measurement instruments and 1 behavioral indicator. The named instruments are SOTOPIA, used in one paper as a social interaction environment with goals, and TTT-Bench, a benchmark of two-player Tic-Tac-Toe-style games introduced to evaluate basic strategic, spatial, and logical reasoning. Together these instruments operationalize the construct in social-goal and adversarial game settings, especially through tasks involving opponents, goals, and move selection for longer-term success. The only linked behavioral indicator is Data analysis, but it appears through a related link rather than as a direct measurement.

**Links.** Beyond measurement, papers report related links rather than causal or correlational ones. One paper relates Data analysis to strategic reasoning by stating that application-driven data analysis requires diverse reasoning skills, including strategic reasoning. Another paper relates Decision-making to strategic reasoning by listing it as one of the abilities evaluated across multi-agent games.
