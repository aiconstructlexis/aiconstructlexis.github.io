**Also known as:** Common sense reasoning, Common-sense reasoning, Commonsense inference, Common sense inference, Common sense understanding, Commonsense Inference, Commonsense grounding, Commonsense understanding  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across these papers, the prevailing usage treats commonsense reasoning as a latent ability to make presumptions, inferences, or judgments about ordinary everyday situations, drawing on general or shared world knowledge rather than only what is explicitly stated. Many definitions add plausible inference, informal reasoning, or filling knowledge gaps with intuition and worldly experience; as one study puts it, "commonsense reasoning relies on informal reasoning to fill in knowledge gaps based on intuition and worldly experience" (ACCORD: Closing the Commonsense Measurability Gap). A smaller line narrows the scope to physical or social situations, cause-and-effect questions, video or spatial interpretation, or embodied task structure, and some papers shift the framing from latent ability to the observable task of answering or selecting plausible options in multiple-choice or true/false form.

**Space of facets.** Papers use several nearby labels, including commonsense inference, commonsense understanding, common sense understanding, commonsense grounding, and common-sense reasoning, often for closely related abilities centered on everyday knowledge and plausible inference. One paper explicitly divides it into explanatory, predictive, and counterfactual sub-skills, while others distinguish physical from social commonsense or situate it in video, spatial, multilingual, and embodied settings; another says its scope is broad and its boundaries are "vague and ill-defined," and introduces a default world model. Across uses, it is commonly grouped with reading comprehension, world knowledge, and broader reasoning, and contrasted with arithmetic, symbolic, logical, mathematical, or scientific reasoning.

**Space of measurement.** Papers measure it with 50 instruments and connect it to 6 behavioral indicators. Benchmark datasets dominate, especially HellaSwag, WinoGrande, PIQA, OpenBookQA, and ARC-Challenge; papers commonly report zero-shot or fine-tuned accuracy on multiple-choice or true/false question answering, sentence completion, disambiguation, and continuation selection, and some also use composite benchmark suites or shared evaluation frameworks. This operationalization mostly matches papers that define commonsense reasoning through question answering or plausible option selection, but it is narrower than definitions that cast it as broader grounding, understanding, or informal reasoning.

**Links.** Beyond measurement, most links are recorded as related rather than causal, placing the construct within broader reasoning families and alongside question answering oriented behaviors. A few papers claim causal relationships from commonsense reasoning to generalization, reward function generation, and hypothesis generation, using language such as models being generalized "owing to the commonsense reasoning faculties" or an LLM leveraging commonsense reasoning "to generate hypotheses." Separate papers claim causal relationships from in-context learning to commonsense reasoning, and from refusal to commonsense reasoning.
