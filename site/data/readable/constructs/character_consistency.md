# Character consistency
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 9  
**Also known as:** Emotional coherence, Behavioral accuracy, Behavioral coherence, Style coherence, Cognitive fidelity  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Most definitions frame this construct as sustained alignment between a character portrayal and the model's responses or actions, but they disagree on what exactly must remain aligned. Some papers define it through emotional expression across a dialogue, some through actions and linguistic patterns matching stated traits, some through actions staying logically consistent with prior behavior and current story context, and some through responses matching the prompt's intended style, tone, and child-directed wording. A smaller line of work uses a broader framing, cognitive fidelity, where the target is faithful simulation of the character's perspective, attitude, psychological state, and decision-making rather than surface imitation.

**Space of facets.** Across the papers, the construct is described through emotional, behavioral, stylistic, and cognitive facets. The behavioral line is itself split between behavioral accuracy, alignment of actions and linguistic patterns with defined personality and traits, and behavioral coherence, logical consistency with previous behaviors and the current context. Other labels, emotional coherence, style coherence, and cognitive fidelity, are used for related facets of the same broader idea.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to this construct, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. Instead, the operationalizations appear as named evaluation dimensions: emotional coherence tracks whether emotional expression stays aligned over a dialogue, behavioral accuracy measures consistency of actions and linguistic patterns with traits, behavioral coherence evaluates consistency with previous behaviors and context, style coherence measures alignment with the character description in prompts, and cognitive fidelity targets perspective, psychological state, and decision-making. These operationalizations largely restate the definitional facets.

**Links.** Papers do not report causal, correlational, or other related links for this construct.

## Sources

**Emotional coherence** (1 paper: [EmoCharacter: Evaluating the Emotional Fidelity of Role-Playing Agents in Dialogues](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.316.pdf))
> The consistency of emotional expression over the course of a dialogue so that responses remain aligned with the character's emotional pattern.

**Behavioral accuracy** (1 paper: [CharacterBox: Evaluating the Role-Playing Capabilities of LLMs in Text-Based Virtual Worlds](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.323.pdf))
> The consistency of a character's actions and linguistic patterns with their defined personality and traits.

**Behavioral coherence** (1 paper: [CharacterBox: Evaluating the Role-Playing Capabilities of LLMs in Text-Based Virtual Worlds](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.323.pdf))
> The logical consistency of a character's actions in relation to their previous behaviors and the current context of the story.

**Style coherence** (1 paper: [MADial-Bench: Towards Real-world Evaluation of Memory-Augmented Dialogue Generation](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.499.pdf))
> The degree to which a response matches the intended character style, tone, and child-directed wording specified by the prompt.

**Cognitive fidelity** (1 paper: [R-CHAR: A Metacognition-Driven Framework for Role-Playing in Large Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1372.pdf))
> The extent to which a model authentically simulates a character’s perspective, psychological state, and decision-making processes rather than merely mimicking surface traits.
