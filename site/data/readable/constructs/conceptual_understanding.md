# Conceptual understanding
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 3  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, conceptual understanding is treated as a latent or underlying grasp of concepts. One framing defines it as the ability to align a model's internal representation of abstract concepts with human expert definitions through structured taxonomy learning, while another defines it as a true and generalizable grasp shown by consistent and correct application across varied tasks in a way that mirrors human use. The common ground is concept-level alignment or grasp, and the difference is whether the emphasis falls on internal alignment to expert definitions or on cross-task application.

**Space of facets.** Systematization is limited, but one paper organizes the construct through sub-concept taxonomy extraction, describing concept alignment as aligning understanding between the model and the expert and using the resulting taxonomy as task instruction for zero-shot learning. Another paper treats success on concept-requiring questions as evidence of broader conceptual understanding, and a separate measurement claim distinguishes conceptual understanding from linguistic competence and general world knowledge by assigning them to different assessments.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is sparse in the recorded links: papers link conceptual understanding to 1 measurement instrument and no behavioral indicators. The only linked instrument is GLUE, which one paper says assesses conceptual understanding. Beyond that explicit link, another paper treats success on structured questions, including AP exams, AIME math competitions, and coding challenges, as evidence of broader conceptual understanding, because those questions are described as requiring conceptual understanding for high scores. These operationalizations are task-performance based, while one definition instead emphasizes alignment of internal representations with expert definitions.
