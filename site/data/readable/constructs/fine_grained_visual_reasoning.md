# Fine-grained visual reasoning
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 3  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the definitions, the prevailing framing is an ability to reason over detailed visual content rather than only coarse scene content. Papers describe it in terms of object attributes, object relationships, and other subtle or intricate image features, and one study explicitly characterizes the relevant content as "object attributes, object relationships, and intricate visual features" (Enhancing Large Vision-Language Models with Ultra-Detailed Image Caption Generation). A closely aligned wording emphasizes complex reasoning that depends on precise understanding of small or detailed visual elements.

**Space of facets.** Papers do not lay out formal subtypes, but they recurrently organize the construct around a few facets: detailed visual attributes, object relations, and small or subtle visual elements. The main contrast is with reasoning over only coarse scene content, so the construct is used for cases where precise local detail matters.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument or behavioral indicator is linked to it. In the quoted usage, authors point to tasks that require fine-grained visual reasoning and to examples such as correctly identifying a person's hair color, but no explicit benchmark or other linked instrument is named.

**Links.** A small amount of relational evidence connects it to another construct: one paper reports that fine-grained visual reasoning is related to robustness. The claim appears in the context of improving LVLM robustness in fine-grained visual reasoning, and no causal or correlational link is recorded.
