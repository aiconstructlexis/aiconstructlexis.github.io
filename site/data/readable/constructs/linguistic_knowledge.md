# Linguistic knowledge
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 4  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across papers, the prevailing usage treats linguistic knowledge as underlying or internalized language information, proficiency, or understanding. Most definitions describe it as what enables interpretation or generation, but they differ in scope: some tie it to generating or aligning text from sign language inputs and to using gloss or text data, some define it as understanding and effectively using spoken language with lexical, syntactic, and semantic coherence, and another paper frames it as a latent, structured understanding that supports morphological, syntactic, semantic, and pragmatic phenomena in LLMs.

**Space of facets.** The papers systematize linguistic knowledge mainly by linguistic components and input modality. Some definitions name lexical knowledge, syntax, and semantic coherence, while another extends the set to morphological, syntactic, semantic, and pragmatic phenomena; a separate usage centers sign language processing and textual alignment or generation, and some authors explicitly describe the knowledge as embedded within or intrinsically represented in the model.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is narrow in the papers: linguistic knowledge is linked to one measurement instrument, BLiMP, and to no behavioral indicators. In the recorded use, one paper evaluates "performance on linguistic knowledge (BLiMP)," so the operationalization is performance-focused, whereas several definitions describe internalized, underlying, or latent knowledge.

**Links.** The relationship evidence is limited to measurement: one paper reports that linguistic knowledge is measured by BLiMP. The papers do not report causal, correlational, or broader related links involving this construct.
