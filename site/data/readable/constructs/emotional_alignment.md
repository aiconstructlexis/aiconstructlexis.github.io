# Emotional alignment
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 6  
**Also known as:** Emotional calibration  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the definitions, the prevailing framing treats emotional alignment as the degree to which an LLM's emotional responses to specific situations correspond with those of humans. A broader framing uses the same label for whether an AI's responses are affectively appropriate and supportive in emotionally sensitive conversations, including avoiding emotional destabilization and using "tone moderation and content adjustment" (EmoAgent: Assessing and Safeguarding Human-AI Interaction for Mental Health Safety). A smaller line of work shifts to "emotional calibration", defining the construct as responses tuned to a user's emotional complexity and context rather than generic or overly affirmative, and describing failures as "surface-level rather than context-specific empathy".

**Space of facets.** Papers do not present a settled subtype structure, but they do organize the construct around a few recurring facets: correspondence with human emotion appraisal, affective appropriateness and supportiveness in sensitive exchanges, and context-sensitive handling of nuanced, multi-layered emotions such as anger, guilt, or relational tension. The main naming variation is between emotional alignment and emotional calibration, with the latter used for a narrower emphasis on tuning responses to context and avoiding generic or overly affirmative replies.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to it, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. Operationally, papers still describe what they look for: one study says it evaluates "emotion appraisal" on different LLMs, while others focus on whether responses provide advisory support, show tone moderation and content adjustment, and handle nuanced emotions rather than defaulting to generic affirmation or surface-level empathy. These descriptions mirror the definitions by focusing on human correspondence in some papers and on appropriateness or context-sensitive tuning in others.
