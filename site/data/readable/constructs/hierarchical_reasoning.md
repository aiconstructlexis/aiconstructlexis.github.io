# Hierarchical reasoning
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 4  
**Also known as:** Hierarchical thinking  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the definitions, the shared framing is multi-step reasoning with hierarchical organization. One usage defines it as the ability to solve problems that require multiple dependent reasoning steps organized in a hierarchy or sequence, while another, under the aligned label "hierarchical thinking," defines it as a latent LLM ability to decompose complex reasoning problems into multiple levels of sub-tasks through a divide-and-conquer strategy for organized planning. As one study puts it, it is "a multi-level divide-and-conquer approach that facilitates deeper and more organized reasoning" (HyperTree Planning: Enhancing LLM Reasoning via Hierarchical Thinking), so the main difference is whether papers frame it as solving hierarchically structured problems or as a latent decomposition and planning ability of the model.

**Space of facets.** Papers mainly systematize the construct through two closely aligned labels, "hierarchical reasoning" and "hierarchical thinking." The fuller elaboration is the divide-and-conquer version, described as decomposing complex problems across multiple levels of sub-tasks, reducing the length of reasoning chains, and maintaining the integrity of the planning process, while the broader formulation centers on tasks whose solutions require dependent reasoning steps arranged hierarchically or sequentially.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to it, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. Papers therefore do not connect it to any named instrument or indicator here, and instead describe it through qualitative characterizations such as solving problems with multiple dependent hierarchical steps or decomposing problems into multiple levels of sub-tasks.

**Links.** The only recorded link is that it is related to System 2 thinking. One paper, Visual Agents as Fast and Slow Thinkers, states that "In System 2, FaST uses many visual agents to accomplish hierarchical reasoning," presenting this as a related relationship rather than a causal or correlational claim.
