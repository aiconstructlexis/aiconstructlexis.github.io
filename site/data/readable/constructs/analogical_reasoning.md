# Analogical reasoning
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 20  
**Also known as:** Analogy-making  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the papers, the dominant framing treats analogical reasoning as a cognitive or higher-order ability to identify structural correspondences or relational patterns and transfer them from a known, source, or reference domain to a new target or application problem. Many definitions repeat the same elements, identifying, mapping, and applying abstract relations, often with transfer to novel settings or concepts. A smaller line of work shifts the emphasis to a latent model ability, where the model draws on relevant past experiences, recalled problems, or self-generated exemplars and applies their solution patterns. The terminology is not fully uniform, since some authors use analogy-making for a closely matching process, and the scope ranges from visual and multimodal cases to concept relations and cultural pattern-matching.

**Space of facets.** Where papers break the construct down, they describe it as involving perceptual understanding, mapping abstract relationships between visual contents, and transferring relational patterns to novel cases. Some papers also place it inside broader reasoning taxonomies: one treats analogical reasoning as one of four logical reasoning types, alongside deductive, inductive, and abductive reasoning, and another uses Analogy as one of three reasoning categories for cultural pattern-matching. Naming also varies, with analogy-making used alongside analogical reasoning, and one paper describes Bongard problems as requiring both visual reasoning and verbal description.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is narrow in the linked evidence: papers connect analogical reasoning to 2 measurement instruments and 1 behavioral indicator. The named instruments are ARC-Challenge, described as a 4-choice format that centers on analogical and inductive reasoning, and DRISHTIKON, where Analogy is a reasoning category framed as cultural pattern-matching; both are task-based instruments rather than human evaluation or probe-style analyses. The linked behavioral indicator is Image generation, in tasks where the aim is to generate an image that completes an analogy problem through perceptual and relational reasoning. Taken together, these measures make the construct observable through performance on analogy questions and analogy-completion tasks, which matches the prevailing definitions' focus on identifying relations and transferring them to new cases.

**Links.** Beyond measurement, papers link analogical reasoning to 6 constructs. Individual papers claim causal relationships from analogical reasoning to Complex reasoning, via a framework proposed to enhance complex reasoning ability, and to Spatial reasoning, where text-based analogical reasoning is said to enhance global scene understanding and spatial reasoning. Other papers report it as related to In-context learning, when recalling relevant problems and solutions in context guides models to solve new problems, to Visual perception, described as one of the atomic abilities involved, to Logical reasoning, where it appears as one of four reasoning types, and to Fluid intelligence, where analogy-making is described as tightly linked to applying learned skills in novel settings. It is also related to the behavioral indicator Image generation, and no correlational relationship is reported.
