# Depth
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 3  
**Also known as:** Depth of knowledge  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the definitions, depth is treated as a property of model outputs: how fully and substantively a response develops its topic, rather than remaining shallow. One definition describes it as generated content that develops topics in a detailed, substantive, and non-shallow way, while another uses the variant name depth of knowledge for the degree to which a response demonstrates substantive expertise or detailed subject-matter knowledge. The shared core is detail and substance in responses, though the emphasis differs between topic development and demonstrated expertise.

**Space of facets.** Systematization is limited. One paper places depth alongside relevance, breadth, and novelty as parallel evaluation dimensions, while another discusses depth of knowledge together with expertise and reports that both humans and LLMs assign high weight to those attributes. The material does not describe subtypes of depth or a broader taxonomy beyond these neighboring terms.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to depth, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. In the uses, papers apply it directly as an evaluation criterion for generated content, for example in a scheme that evaluates relevance, breadth, depth, and novelty, and as a named preference attribute to which humans and LLMs can assign high weight. These uses also treat depth as something assessed from responses or generated content.
