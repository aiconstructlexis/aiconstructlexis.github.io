# Fluid intelligence
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 3  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** In the usage in the papers, fluid intelligence is treated as a latent ability for solving novel problems without relying on prior or pre-existing knowledge or memorization. The definition also specifies abstract inductive reasoning and pattern induction from examples, and frames evaluating fluid intelligence as a way to assess LLM reasoning abilities given their exposure to and memorization of large amounts of knowledge during pre-training.

**Space of facets.** The construct is organized mainly by contrast: it is described as solving new problems without relying on prior knowledge or memorization, and as involving abstract inductive reasoning and pattern induction from examples. A nearby construct is analogical reasoning, which one paper describes as tightly linked to fluid intelligence.

**Space of measurement.** It is linked to 1 measurement instrument and no behavioral indicators. The only linked instrument is the Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus, which one paper calls the de facto standard for measuring machine fluid intelligence, so the recorded operationalization is performance on the ARC task.

**Links.** One paper reports that analogical reasoning is related to fluid intelligence, stating that analogy-making is tightly linked with fluid intelligence and characterizing fluid intelligence as the capacity to apply learned skills in novel settings.
