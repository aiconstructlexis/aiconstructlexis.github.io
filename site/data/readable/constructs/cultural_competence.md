# Cultural competence
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 12  
**Also known as:** Cultural competency, Cultural sensitivity  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the definitions, the prevailing usage treats cultural competence as a broad model capability: understanding diverse cultural contexts and navigating or engaging with them appropriately. A common overlapping framing, often under the label cultural sensitivity, narrows this to acknowledging and respecting differences in values, norms, communication styles, and practices so that outputs are not offensive, alienating, inappropriate, or biased. A smaller line of work uses the term for how well models capture culturally nuanced knowledge across cultural groups and prompt conditions, so the clearest variation is between broad cross-cultural engagement, output-level appropriateness, and knowledge accuracy; some papers describe it as a latent model ability, while others define it through model outputs.

**Space of facets.** Papers use cultural competence, cultural competency, and cultural sensitivity in overlapping ways rather than as sharply separated terms. The content grouped under the construct includes behavioral norms, linguistic conventions, values, communication styles, practices, and taboos, and it is also specialized to domains such as non-verbal gestures, food-related cultural knowledge, multilingual expressions and slurs, and multimodal content. One paper further states that cultural competence is not a generalizable objective, which marks a narrower way of framing the construct.

**Space of measurement.** No paper links cultural competence to a measurement instrument or behavioral indicator. Even so, papers describe evaluating it through cultural competence evaluation, assessment of sensitivity toward Persian culture, probing of food-related cultural knowledge across cultural groups and prompt conditions, tests of region-specific or offensive non-verbal gesture interpretation, and toxicity evaluation in multilingual low-resource settings that focus on culturally specific expressions, slurs, and social group dynamics. These operationalizations target the same dimensions named in the definitions: appropriate response to cultural norms and taboos, culturally relevant outputs, and capture of nuanced cultural knowledge.
