# Self-preference bias
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 7  
**Also known as:** Self-preference, Model bias, Self-enhancement bias  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the definitions, the prevailing usage treats self-preference bias as a judging bias: a latent tendency for an LLM serving as a judge to rate its own responses more favorably than other models' responses, or more highly than their actual quality warrants. A smaller line of work broadens it into model bias in self-referential evaluation, where generation or evaluation aligns with a model's own implicit strengths and gives it an unfair advantage. Another minority framing extends the preference beyond exact self-outputs to outputs from models in the same family or with similar training patterns, so the main disagreement is about scope, from strict self-favoring to affinity for related styles or strengths.

**Space of facets.** Papers do not offer a detailed internal taxonomy, but they do use several near-synonymous labels for overlapping phenomena. Self-preference bias and self-enhancement bias are used for judges favoring their own answers, while model bias names a broader unfairness tied to a model's implicit strengths during self-referential evaluation. The construct is therefore positioned variously as a latent judging tendency, as evaluation unfairness, and in one account as familiarity with closely related models' response style and content.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to it, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. Even so, papers do describe concrete operationalizations: one defines the DBG score as the difference between the scores a judge gives to its own responses and the corresponding gold judgments, and other quoted passages characterize the behavior as judges favoring their own answers or rating their own outputs more favorably. This operationalization follows the narrower definitions centered on judges preferring their own outputs, while the broader accounts based on implicit strengths or same-family similarity are described conceptually rather than through linked instruments.

## Sources

**Self-preference bias** (2 papers: [Beyond the Surface: Measuring Self-Preference in LLM Judgments](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.86.pdf), [An Empirical Analysis of Uncertainty in Large Language Model Evaluations](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/82d3258eb58ceac31744a88005b7ddef-Paper-Conference.pdf))
> The latent tendency of a large language model to assign higher scores to its own generated responses when serving as a judge, independent of the actual quality of those responses.

**Model bias** (1 paper: [Unbiased Evaluation of Large Language Models from a Causal Perspective](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v267/main/assets/chen25bi/chen25bi.pdf))
> The tendency of a model to generate or evaluate content that aligns with its own implicit strengths, giving itself an unfair advantage during self-referential evaluation processes.

**Self-enhancement bias** (1 paper: [Justice or Prejudice? Quantifying Biases in LLM-as-a-Judge](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/fdca08d371e4b6c031397909e20043bd-Paper-Conference.pdf))
> A tendency for an LLM judge to favor responses generated by itself over responses generated by other models.
