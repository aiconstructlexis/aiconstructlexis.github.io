# Multimodal perception
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 6  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the definitions, the prevailing usage treats multimodal perception as a latent ability to comprehend and interpret information by integrating visual and textual inputs. Most descriptions keep this broad, ranging from awareness of agent status, surroundings, and objects to unified processing of interleaved images and text; one paper calls it "the most basic ability" for multimodal agents, and another says visual comprehension "lays the foundation for subsequent interactions with the world." A narrower framing appears in chart work, where it is defined as extracting and integrating visual and textual information from charts, including axes, legends, colors, structures, and spatial relationships, so the main variation is broad environment-level perception versus task-specific chart perception, and agent-centered versus model-centered wording.

**Space of facets.** When papers subdivide it, they do so through task groupings such as general, knowledge, OCR, and vision-centric perception. Other papers specify the content it covers rather than distinct subconstructs: status and environmental features, surroundings and objects, or chart elements such as axes, legends, colors, structures, and spatial relationships. No construct links are given that equate it with, nest it under, or contrast it against another construct.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is mainly benchmark-based: the construct is linked to 14 measurement instruments and 1 behavioral indicator. The most frequently linked instruments listed are MME, AI2D, ChartQA, DocVQA, and GQA, plus 9 other instruments, and one paper explicitly measures it through general, knowledge, OCR, and vision-centric tasks. This measurement space largely follows the definitions' emphasis on integrating visual and textual information, and the chart-specific framing also appears in measurement through ChartQA; the sole linked behavioral indicator is chart question answering.

**Links.** Beyond measurement links, the papers do not report causal or correlational relationships for this construct. One paper relates chart question answering to multimodal perception, describing chart question answering as a multimodal task used to evaluate reasoning capabilities, especially multimodal perception ability and local reasoning ability.
