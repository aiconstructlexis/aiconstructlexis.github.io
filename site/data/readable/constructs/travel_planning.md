# Travel planning
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 4  
**Also known as:** Travel plan generation, All-in-one travel plan generation  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the definitions, the prevailing usage treats travel planning as generating a trip itinerary. Papers agree on itinerary creation as the core, but vary in scope: some describe a basic form as producing a travel itinerary with arrangements of points of interest, while others use a broader framing that includes timing, attractions, dining, accommodations, tickets, and costs, or a detailed itinerary covering transportation, accommodation, and activities under constraints such as budget and time. One definition also frames it as an observable action, while another presents it as a task of producing a plan that satisfies humans' constraints.

**Space of facets.** Papers distinguish travel planning most clearly by how comprehensive the itinerary must be. One line separates "travel plan generation" from "all-in-one travel plan generation," contrasting basic point-of-interest arrangements with fuller plans that add timing, dining, accommodations, tickets, and costs. Another formulation centers the concept on constraint satisfaction, where the plan is produced from a natural language description of constraints and is expected to satisfy them.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to travel planning, and no behavioral indicator is linked to it. Instead, papers operationalize it through task formulations: a system is asked to output a travel plan, in one case from "a natural language description of humans' constraints C" that the plan should satisfy, and in another as either a basic itinerary or a more comprehensive all-in-one itinerary.

**Links.** One paper relates constraint satisfaction to travel planning, and the recorded link is related rather than causal or correlational. In that usage, travel planning is connected to generating feasible travel plans that "holistically satisfy constraints."
