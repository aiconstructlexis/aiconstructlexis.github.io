# Statistical reasoning
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 3  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the cited work, the dominant framing treats statistical reasoning as the ability to reason over quantitative information and statistical relationships. One definition describes it as proficiency in quantitative synthesis across multiple documents, including aggregation, distributional analysis, and correlation analysis, while another frames it as interpreting charts, plots, and tables to understand trends, distributions, and statistical relationships. The main variation is the context of use, multi-document synthesis versus visually presented data, rather than whether statistical analysis is involved.

**Space of facets.** Papers place it inside broader reasoning taxonomies rather than identifying it with another construct. One benchmark uses it as one of three primary categories and describes statistical tasks through aggregation, correlation analysis, and variance analysis across multiple documents, while another treats it as one of seven mathematical reasoning types and situates figure question answering around statistical reasoning over multiple charts and plots. No construct-to-construct links are recorded for it.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is benchmark-based: papers link it to 2 measurement instruments and 3 behavioral indicators. The linked instruments are CLIMATEVIZ, a benchmark for statistical reasoning and fact verification on scientific charts, and MEBench, which includes a statistical reasoning category within cross-document multi-entity question answering; the named behavioral indicators are Aggregative question answering, Correlation analysis, and Variance analysis. These measures closely follow the two main definitions, with one instrument centered on charts and the other on multi-document quantitative synthesis.

**Links.** No causal or correlational claims are recorded. Instead, papers report related links connecting Aggregative question answering to Statistical reasoning, Correlation analysis to Statistical reasoning, and Statistical reasoning to Variance analysis, using the same language that describes statistical tasks as quantitative synthesis across multiple documents.
