# Theory of mind
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 33  
**Also known as:** Theory of Mind, Belief modeling, Mental state reasoning, Belief formation, Action intention inference, Mental state tracking, Multi-agent mental state tracking, Intentionality understanding, Perspective separation  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the surveyed work, the prevailing usage treats theory of mind as a latent model ability to attribute, infer, track, or reason about other agents' mental states, especially beliefs, intentions, desires, knowledge, and sometimes preferences, often so the model can predict behavior or act effectively in negotiation, coordination, communication, or other social settings. Many definitions use closely matching language about understanding that others can hold mental states different from one's own, and some quoted passages make this concrete as estimating an opposing party's payoff table or handling information asymmetry and secret-keeping. A smaller line of work narrows the construct to belief or role modeling, belief formation about likely actions, action intention inference from observed behavior, intentionality understanding, or internal perspective separation, so the main variation is between broad mental-state reasoning and narrower subskills.

**Space of facets.** Papers systematize the construct through subskills and neighboring labels. Some split it into mental state tracking, multi-agent mental state tracking in triadic or tetradic interactions, and a pairing of perspective separation with belief inference; others use labels such as belief modeling, mental state reasoning, belief formation, intentionality understanding, and action intention inference for narrower formulations centered on hidden roles, likely actions, goals, desires, or action semantics. In relational descriptions, perspective taking is treated as foundational within theory of mind, state tracking as a skill underlying theory-of-mind reasoning, and theory of mind as tied to broader social intelligence and social reasoning.

**Space of measurement.** Papers link theory of mind to 5 measurement instruments and 2 behavioral indicators. MMToM-QA and ToM-SSI are described as evaluation benchmarks, FANToM as a prominent theory-of-mind benchmark, TactfulToM as a three-tier evaluation framework whose info-state questions assess basic mental state tracking, and MuSR as a reasoning setting whose domains include social situations and theory-of-mind. The behavioral indicators concern generating natural-language hypotheses about other agents' latent variables and state tracking, so the operationalizations mostly probe inference or tracking of beliefs, knowledge, intentions, and perspectives in multimodal, narrative, or situated-interaction tasks.

**Links.** Papers claim a causal relationship from theory of mind to hypothesis generation, planning, and pragmatic understanding: one ToM module is said to generate hypotheses about other agents' latent variables and scaffold high-level planning, and another paper states that pragmatic inferences often rely on ToM abilities. No correlational link is reported. Papers also relate theory of mind to social intelligence and perspective taking, as well as to other constructs about belief and social reasoning and to a state-tracking skill; the recorded language presents perspective taking as foundational within ToM and state tracking as underlying theory-of-mind reasoning.
