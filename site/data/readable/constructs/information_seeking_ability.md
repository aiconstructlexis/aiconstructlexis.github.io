# Information-seeking ability
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 4  
**Also known as:** Information seeking  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across the definitions, the prevailing framing is a latent model capability for proactive question-asking: identifying knowledge gaps, eliciting missing details, and gathering the information needed to make more informed decisions or solve a task, especially under uncertainty and ambiguity. The papers also describe success in turn-by-turn terms, with an information-seeking expert deciding whether it already has enough information to answer confidently. A smaller usage applies the label "information seeking" to a task category, namely responding to queries that ask for factual information or explanations, so the main disagreement is between a model ability and a type of query.

**Space of facets.** There is little formal subdivision. Papers mostly organize the construct around question-asking ability, active information-seeking capabilities, and proactive information-seeking settings, all centered on asking effective questions to obtain missing details; one description of a successful information-seeking expert emphasizes deciding at each turn whether further questions are needed before answering. The main naming ambiguity is that the same label is also used in one paper for information-seeking questions as a task category rather than for the underlying latent ability.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument and no behavioral indicator are linked to the construct. Even so, the descriptions make the operationalization concrete through question-asking behavior: one paper introduces a benchmark to evaluate question-asking ability in LLMs, and another notes that some methods do not evaluate models' active information-seeking capabilities. This operationalization lines up with the dominant definitions, which frame the construct as asking questions to gather missing information.

**Links.** The only linked construct is Persuasiveness. One paper claims a causal relationship from Persuasiveness to Information-seeking ability, stating that interviewer LLMs struggle with engaging persuasively, which leads to suboptimal information extraction across model size and capability. No correlational or related links are reported.
