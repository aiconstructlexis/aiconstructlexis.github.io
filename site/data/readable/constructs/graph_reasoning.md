# Graph reasoning
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 11  
**Also known as:** Graph reasoning ability  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across papers, the prevailing usage treats graph reasoning as a latent ability or facet of reasoning for understanding and solving problems on graph-structured data. Definitions commonly describe inferring graph properties such as paths, connectivity, or cycles, and one definition broadens this to understanding, retaining, and manipulating relations among entities across tasks. A narrower framing places it under algorithmic reasoning, while a smaller and distinct usage applies the same name to interpreting data in graphical or chart form to answer a question.

**Space of facets.** Papers most often describe graph reasoning through families of graph problems. One line treats it as a specific facet of algorithmic reasoning, and one paper spells out graph reasoning tasks through graph connectivity, shortest path, and cycle detection; other definitions emphasize identifying nodes, edges, and constraints and applying appropriate graph algorithms. A smaller strand uses graph recall, described as recalling and encoding relations given earlier in text, as a simple task for studying graph reasoning ability.

**Space of measurement.** No measurement instrument is linked to graph reasoning, while three behavioral indicators and two constructs are linked. The linked behavioral indicators are graph connectivity, graph partitioning, and shortest path finding, and quoted passages also describe operationalization through graph recall, broader graph problems, more intricate graph tasks, and changes in graph description order that affect graph reasoning performance. Overall, the recorded operationalization is task-based and centered on solving graph problems or recalling graph-structured relations.

**Links.** Only related links are recorded. Papers relate algorithmic reasoning, graph connectivity, and graph partitioning to graph reasoning, and separate papers relate graph reasoning to shortest path finding and to zero-shot generalization, including a claim that graph reasoning capabilities show strong zero-shot generalizability to unseen datasets and knowledge graphs without additional training. No causal or correlational relationship is recorded.
