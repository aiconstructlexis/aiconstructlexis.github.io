# Social intelligence
**Type:** Construct  
**Supporting papers:** 14  

## State of the Field

**Space of definitions.** Across papers, the prevailing usage treats social intelligence as a latent capacity or ability to understand and navigate complex social interactions or environments. Most definitions combine pursuit of social goals with inference about others, variously framed as others' goals, mental states, intentions, emotions, behaviors, or private information, and several also include adherence to norms or social rules and maintaining relationships. A smaller line of work uses the same label more behaviorally, referring to realistic role enactment and collaboration, or more narrowly to making effective decisions and optimizing outcomes through interaction with other agents in cooperative contexts.

**Space of facets.** Papers break the construct into recurring facets rather than a single fixed core: goal completion, information or implicit reasoning, inference or safeguarding of private information, response to others' emotions and intentions, adherence to norms, and collaborative behavior. Some papers explicitly position theory of mind, reasoning about others' intentions, goals, thoughts, and beliefs, as a foundation of social intelligence, and another treats social reasoning as a core competency within it. The same label is therefore used both for broad navigation of dynamic social contexts and for narrower targets such as cooperative outcome optimization or role-based collaboration.

**Space of measurement.** Operationalization is benchmark-centered: papers link social intelligence to 3 measurement instruments and 1 behavioral indicator. The linked instruments are SOTOPIA, listed most often and described as an open-ended environment or benchmark for simulating complex social interactions between artificial agents, SIQA for social awareness capabilities, and ToMBench for emotionally and socially grounded reasoning such as emotion recognition, social action prediction, and theory-of-mind reasoning; another paper also describes evaluating social intelligence through multi-turn interactions that emphasize goal completion and implicit reasoning. The only linked behavioral indicator is coalition formation, so the measured space spans interactive social tasks and benchmark tests of social awareness and reasoning.

**Links.** Beyond measurement, the recorded links are relational rather than causal or correlational. Social intelligence is related to theory of mind, described as reasoning about others' intentions, goals, thoughts, and beliefs, and to social reasoning, described as a core competency of social intelligence. Other papers relate reasoning and implicit reasoning to social intelligence, and coalition formation is related to the quantitative study of social intelligence.
